# Professional competence for AFS compilation: mapping to the International Education Standards (IES)

> Source: IFAC, *Handbook of International Education Pronouncements*, 2026 edition (Framework 2015; IES 2, 3, 4
> and 6 (2026); IES 5 (2015); IES 7 (2020); IES 8 (2021)). Learning outcomes below are quoted word for word from
> each standard's Table A. **Mapping outcomes to compilation steps is this file's own judgement.** The handbook
> does not mention AFS compilation, the IFRS for SMEs Standard, directors' reports or foreign currency translation.
> Where it says nothing on a point, the file says "Source silent".

## 1. Orientation: what each standard covers (2026 status)

The Framework says professional competence "goes beyond knowledge of principles, standards, concepts, facts, and
procedures; it is the integration and application of (a) technical competence, (b) professional skills, and
(c) professional values, ethics, and attitudes" (Framework 18; IES 2.A5). Those three elements are covered by
IES 2, 3 and 4. IES 6 sets out how they are assessed.

| Standard | Covers | Status in the 2026 handbook |
|---|---|---|
| IES 2 Technical Competence | 12 technical competence areas, each with a required proficiency level, to be reached by the end of Initial Professional Development (IPD) (IES 2.1, 2.7, Table A) | Revised (2026). Effective 1 July 2026 (2.5). Sustainability concepts are embedded throughout. Adds a **new assurance competence area at foundation level** and gives business acumen more weight (Handbook "Changes of Substance") |
| IES 3 Professional Skills | Intellectual, interpersonal and communication, personal, and organizational skills (3.1, Table A) | Revised (2026). Effective 1 July 2026 (3.5). Sustainability embedded; enabling behavioural competences updated |
| IES 4 Professional Values, Ethics, and Attitudes | Professional scepticism and professional judgement, ethical principles, and commitment to the public interest (4.11, Table A). Reflective activity must be formalized and documented (4.13) | Revised (2026). Effective 1 July 2026 (4.7) |
| IES 5 Practical Experience | Practical experience during IPD, measured on an output-based, input-based or combination approach (5.12–15). Supervision by a practical experience supervisor (5.16) | 2015. Effective 1 July 2015 (5.8). Conforming amendments only |
| IES 6 Formal Assessment of Professional Competence | Principles for designing, delivering and overseeing assessment (6.9). Assessment must rest on verifiable evidence (6.10) | Revised (2026). Effective 1 July 2026 (6.6). Modernized for hybrid and remote assessment. Adds two new principles, **integrity and authenticity**, and updates **equity** |
| IES 7 Continuing Professional Development | CPD after IPD to develop and maintain competence. Measured on an output-based approach, an input-based approach, or both (7.9, 7.12) | 2020. Effective 1 January 2020 (7.7) |
| IES 8 Engagement Partners (audits of FS) | Competence of an audit engagement partner (8.7–8, Table A) | 2021. Effective 1 January 2021 (8.6) |

IES 8 is relevant here only as the **reviewer's benchmark**. For example, its Table A (b)(iii) reads "Evaluate
accounting judgments and estimates, including fair value estimates, made by management". Its (b)(iv) reads
"Evaluate the fair presentation of financial statements relative to the nature of the business, the operating
environment, and the entity's ability to continue as a going concern." This is the standard of scrutiny that
compiled AFS will face at audit.

## 2. Proficiency levels (IES 2 Appendix 1; same text in IES 3 and IES 4 Appendix 1)

Each level "describes the context in which the relevant learning outcomes are expected to be achieved". It is
based on "the ambiguity, complexity, and uncertainty of the work environment" (Framework 22).

| Level | The handbook's description (condensed; key phrases quoted) | Work environment |
|---|---|---|
| Foundation | "Defining, explaining, summarizing, and interpreting the underlying principles…while working under appropriate supervision". "Performing assigned tasks". "Solving simple problems, and referring complex tasks or problems to supervisors" | "low levels of ambiguity, complexity, and uncertainty" |
| Intermediate | "Independently applying, comparing, and analyzing underlying principles…to complete work assignments and make decisions". "Combining technical competence and professional skills". "Applying professional values, ethics, and attitudes". Presenting "to accounting and non-accounting stakeholders" | "moderate levels" |
| Advanced | "Selecting and integrating principles and theories from different areas…to manage and lead projects". "Making judgments on appropriate courses of action". "Assessing, researching, and resolving complex problems with limited supervision". "Anticipating, consulting appropriately…". Presenting "in a persuasive manner" | "high levels" |

**Levels prescribed by the end of IPD:**
- IES 2: (a) Financial accounting and reporting, Intermediate. (b) Management accounting, Intermediate.
  (c) Finance and financial management, Intermediate. (d) Taxation, Intermediate. (e) Assurance, Foundation.
  (f) Audit, Intermediate. (g) Governance, risk management and internal control, Intermediate. (h) Business laws
  and regulations, Intermediate. (i) ICT, Intermediate. (j) Business and organizational context, Intermediate.
  (k) Economics, Foundation. (l) Business strategy and management, Intermediate.
- IES 3: all four areas are Intermediate (3.A14).
- IES 4: all three areas are Intermediate (4.A22).

No competence area is set at Advanced by the end of IPD. Member bodies may raise levels or add outcomes
(Framework 23; IES 2.A15).

## 3. Quoted outcomes used in the mapping

**IES 2 Table A**
- **(a) Financial accounting and reporting (Intermediate)**
  - (i) "Apply accounting principles to transactions and other events and conditions based on facts and circumstances."
  - (ii) "Apply International Financial Reporting Standards (IFRSs) or other relevant standards to transactions and other events and conditions based on facts and circumstances."
  - (iii) "Evaluate the appropriateness of policies used to prepare general purpose financial reports."
  - (iv) "Evaluate connected information when preparing general purpose financial reports."
  - (v) "Prepare general purpose financial reports, including consolidated financial statements, in accordance with IFRSs or other relevant standards."
  - (vi) "Interpret general purpose financial reports."
- **(d) Taxation (Intermediate)**
  - (ii) "Prepare direct and indirect tax calculations for individuals and organizations."
  - (iii) "Analyze the taxation issues associated with non-complex international transactions."
- **(e) Assurance (Foundation)**
  - (iv) "Explain the concept of material misstatement and its effect on an assurance engagement."
  - (vi) "Identify relevant and reliable evidence to form judgments, make informed decisions, and reach well-reasoned conclusions."
  - (vii) "Explain how contradictory assurance evidence may affect judgments, decisions, and conclusions."
- **(c) Finance and financial management (Intermediate)**
  - (ii) "Analyze an organization's cash flow and working capital requirements."
  - (iii) "Analyze the current and anticipated financial performance and position of an organization, using techniques including ratio analysis, trend analysis, cash flow analysis, and scenario analysis."
- **(g) Governance, risk management and internal control (Intermediate)**
  - (i) "Explain the principles of good governance, including the rights and responsibilities of owners, investors, and those charged with governance; and the role of stakeholders in governance, disclosure, and transparency requirements."
  - (iv) "Analyze the components of internal control related to external reporting."
  - (v) "Analyze the adequacy of systems, processes and controls for collecting, generating, storing, accessing, using, sharing, or reporting data and information."
- **(h) Business laws and regulations (Intermediate)**
  - (i) "Explain the laws and regulations that govern the different forms of legal entities."
  - (ii) "Explain the laws and regulations applicable to the context in which professional accountants operate."
  - (iii) "Apply data protection and privacy regulations when collecting, generating, storing, accessing, using, sharing, or reporting data and information."
- **(i) ICT (Intermediate)**
  - (iv) "Use ICT to analyze data and information."
  - (vii) "Analyze the adequacy of ICT processes and controls."
- **(j) Business and organizational context (Intermediate)**
  - (ii) "Analyze an organization's business model, including its value chain."
  - (iii) "Analyze geopolitical, economic, social, and other factors that affect international trade and finance."
- **(k) Economics (Foundation)**
  - (ii) "Describe the effect of changes in macroeconomic indicators on business activity."

"Connected information" is defined in IES 2 Table B by reference to IFRS S1 para. 21. "General purpose financial
reports" include "an entity's general purpose financial statements and sustainability-related financial
disclosures" (IES 2.A1 Table B; A16).

**IES 3 Table A (all Intermediate)**
- **(a) Intellectual**
  - (i) "Evaluate data and information from a variety of sources and perspectives through research, collaboration, integration, and analysis."
  - (ii) "Identify when it is appropriate to consult with experts."
  - (iii) "Apply critical thinking skills to solve problems, form judgments, make informed decisions, and reach well-reasoned conclusions."
  - (iv) "Recommend solutions to unstructured, multi-faceted problems."
  - (v) "Evaluate changing facts and circumstances to solve problems, form judgments, make informed decisions, and reach well-reasoned conclusions."
- **(b) Interpersonal and communication**
  - (ii) "Communicate clearly and concisely with a range of stakeholders."
  - (iv) "Apply active listening and effective questioning techniques."
- **(c) Personal**
  - (iii) "Manage time and resources to achieve professional commitments."
  - (iv) "Anticipate challenges and plan potential solutions."
  - (vi) "Identify the potential impact of personal and organizational bias."
- **(d) Organizational**
  - (i) "Undertake assignments in accordance with established practices to meet prescribed deadlines."
  - (ii) "Review own work and that of others to determine whether it complies with an organization's quality standards."

**IES 4 Table A (all Intermediate)**
- **(a) Professional skepticism and professional judgment**
  - (i) "Apply an inquiring mind when collecting and assessing data and information."
  - (ii) "Apply techniques to reduce bias when solving problems, forming judgments, making informed decisions, reaching well-reasoned conclusions, and communicating with a range of stakeholders."
  - (iii) "Apply critical thinking when identifying and evaluating alternatives to determine an appropriate course of action."
- **(b) Ethical principles**
  - (ii) "Apply relevant ethical requirements to all professional activities."
  - (iii) "Apply fundamental principles of ethics when collecting, generating, storing, accessing, using, sharing, or reporting data and information."
  - (iv) "Identify threats to compliance with the fundamental principles of ethics."
  - (v) "Evaluate the significance of threats to compliance with the fundamental principles of ethics and respond appropriately."
- **(c) Commitment to the public interest**
  - (ii) "Explain the role and importance of ethics in relation to business and good governance."
  - (iii) "Analyze the interrelationship of ethics and law, including the relationship between laws, regulations, and the public interest."

Definitions adopted in IES 4 Table B:
- Professional scepticism: "an attitude that includes a questioning mind, being alert to conditions which may indicate possible misstatement, and a critical assessment of evidence" (IAASB).
- Bias in "formal communications (e.g., general-purpose financial reports)": IES 4.A29.

**Note A: where steps exceed Intermediate.** Appendix 1 places "Assessing, researching, and resolving complex
problems with limited supervision" and "Making judgments on appropriate courses of action" at Advanced. Some
compilation work fits that description: significant estimates, going-concern doubt, functional-currency
determination when indicators conflict, and non-routine policy choices. IES 2/3/4 require only Intermediate by
the end of IPD. A newly qualified compiler should therefore consult on these matters, consistent with IES 3
(a)(ii) and IES 4.A34.

## 5. Using this skill for teaching

Formal assessment must have "high levels of" seven principles (IES 6.9, listed alphabetically and "of equal
importance"): authenticity, equity, integrity, reliability, sufficiency, transparency and validity. Integrity and
authenticity were added in 2026. The formats IES 6.A5 allows: written or computer-based examinations,
presentations, case studies, simulations, workplace assessment by employers, and portfolios of evidence. IES 2.A26
and 4.A43 list the same formats. Formal assessment rests on "verifiable evidence", defined as "objective, and
capable of being proven and retained" (6.10, A24).

**Turning the compilation steps into assessed tasks**

| Step(s) | Assessed task (suggested) | Main outcomes assessed |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Role-play an engagement-acceptance meeting and write a memo on scope and threats to compliance with ethics. Role play is a participative approach listed in IES 4.A27(a) | IES 2 (h)(i); IES 4 (b)(iv)–(v); IES 3 (b)(iv) |
| 2–3 | Give students a raw general-ledger export with planted errors. They clean it, reconcile it to the trial balance, and log the exceptions found | IES 2 (i)(iv), (g)(v); IES 4 (a)(i) |
| 4–5 | Mapping schedule plus an adjusting-journal pack. Each journal carries a short rationale citing the IFRS for SMEs paragraph relied on | IES 2 (a)(i)–(ii); IES 3 (a)(iii) |
| 6–7 | Accounting policy and materiality memo, with at least two alternatives evaluated | IES 2 (a)(iii); IES 4 (a)(ii)–(iii) |
| 8 | Translate USD statements into KHR and explain the movement in the translation reserve | IES 2 (a)(ii), (j)(iii) |
| 9–10 | Full draft AFS with notes and a directors' report, then a short oral presentation to "directors" | IES 2 (a)(iv)–(v); IES 3 (b)(ii) |
| 11 | Peer review of another group's AFS against a disclosure checklist, followed by a reflective log. IES 4.13 requires reflective activity that is "formalized and documented", and 4.A39 suggests "Reflective records" | IES 3 (d)(ii); IES 4 (a)(i), (b)(v) |

**Applying each principle (definitions and examples from IES 6.A10–A23)**
- **Authenticity:** the task should reflect "realistic situations that may be faced by professional accountants"
  (A10). A11 suggests three ways to raise it: tasks that resemble a professional accountant's work, technology
  such as "software packages, data analytics and visualization tools", and "real-life case studies". A messy
  general-ledger export and a spreadsheet trial balance meet all three.
- **Equity:** the task should be "fair and without bias" and "accessible and inclusive" (A12). A13 suggests
  providing the technology the assessment relies on, reviewing materials for linguistic and cultural diversity
  (relevant to bilingual English/Khmer cohorts), publishing clear requirements, and making adjustments such as
  extra time.
- **Integrity:** design to minimize "breaches of assessment security, improper administration and/or completion"
  (A14). A15 suggests "variable, comparable test materials", for example a different trial balance or exchange
  rate set for each group. It also suggests originality checks and "clear policy statements…including whether
  artificial intelligence may be used". State that policy explicitly if students may use this skill.
- **Reliability:** the task "consistently produces the same conclusion, given the same set of circumstances". The
  test is whether "the majority of assessors, acting independently, consistently come to the same judgment" (A16).
  A17 suggests a rubric or marking guide with assessor training, unambiguous wording, and a quality review of the
  content before release. Use a model answer with tolerances for judgemental items.
- **Sufficiency:** an "appropriate balance of depth and breadth, knowledge, and application, and integration"
  (A18). Cover IES 2, 3 and 4 outcomes (breadth) at the required depth (A19(a)). The full eleven-step project
  plus a final examination is one way to achieve this.
- **Transparency:** disclose "the competence areas and learning outcomes to be assessed and timing" (A20). A21
  lists the areas to be assessed, formats "including rubric and evaluation criteria", timing, how assessments are
  developed, examiner reports and past questions, and assessment policies. Publish the mapping table in section 4
  with the brief.
- **Validity:** the task "assesses the intended learning outcomes" (A22).
  - Face validity rises with "a comprehensive and relevant case study rather than a simple case study based on
    incomplete information" (A23(a)).
  - Content validity rises when the task covers "more, rather than a few, aspects" (A23(c)).
  - Predictive validity is defined in A22(b).

**Evidence to retain** (6.10, A25): recorded assessment outcomes, marked rubrics and the reflective logs.

**Source silent:** the handbook gives no guidance on grading scales, weightings, or pass marks for university
modules. IES 6 is addressed to IFAC member organizations (6.2). University assessments may feed into formal
assessment as "substantive input" (6.A2).
