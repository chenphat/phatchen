# Section 34 — Specialised Activities (third edition)

> Sources: Educational Module 34 (third edition); IFRS for SMEs Accounting Standard (2025) paragraphs 34.1–34.16, transition A49; Section 30 paragraphs 30.7, 30.9–30.11, 30.18–30.20, 30.26 for the currency section.

Section 34 covers three unrelated activities with no common requirements: **agriculture** (34.2–34.10), **exploration for and evaluation of mineral resources** (34.11–34.11G) and **service concession arrangements** (34.12–34.16). All other relevant sections still apply.

## When this section matters in an SME compilation
- Farms, plantations, livestock, aquaculture: ledger accounts for livestock, growing crops, orchards/vines, harvested produce, fair value gains (34.2–34.10).
- Plantations of bearer plants (e.g. rubber, oil palm, tea, grape vines, cacao, fruit trees) — decide Section 17 vs Section 34 at initial recognition (34.2A).
- Mining / oil and gas entities with licences, exploratory drilling, surveys, sampling (34.11–34.11G).
- Contractors that build and operate public infrastructure (roads, hospitals, energy networks) for a government grantor (34.12–34.16).

## What changed in the third edition
- Bearer plants measurable separately from their produce without undue cost or effort (initially and on an ongoing basis) are accounted for under Section 17; produce remains in Section 34 (34.2–34.2B).
- Fair value measurement guidance removed (now Section 12, 34.6); some biological-asset fair value disclosures relocated to Section 12 (34.7(b) deleted).
- Exploration and evaluation (E&E) assets must be treated as a separate class with Section 17 or Section 18 disclosures (34.11G).
- Transition: bearer plants may be measured at fair value at the beginning of the earliest period presented as deemed cost, difference to opening retained earnings (A49) — relief for entities that held bearer plants at fair value and lack historical cost. Effective for periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027.

## Scope and key definitions
- **Agricultural activity**: management by an entity of the biological transformation of biological assets for sale, into agricultural produce or into additional biological assets. Harvesting from unmanaged sources (ocean fishing, deforestation) and animals for competitions, racing or exhibition are not agricultural activity.
- **Biological asset**: a living animal or plant. **Agricultural produce**: the harvested product of the entity's biological assets. Products from processing after harvest (cheese, wine, lumber, palm oil, rubber products) are outside Section 34 (Section 13).
- **Bearer plant**: a living plant that (a) is used in the production or supply of agricultural produce; (b) is expected to bear produce for more than one period; and (c) has a remote likelihood of being sold as agricultural produce, except incidental scrap sales. Not bearer plants (34.2B): plants cultivated for harvest as produce (lumber trees; trees cultivated for both fruit and lumber) and annual crops (maize, wheat). Bearer **livestock** (dairy cows, breeding animals) is always in Section 34.
- Section 34 applies to **all** biological assets of an entity engaged in agricultural activity (e.g. mules pulling feed carts on a cattle farm, Ex 2), not only those related to the activity. Guard dogs of a security firm and zoo birds held for viewing are PPE (Ex 3, Q7); birds bought for immediate resale are inventory (Q6).
- **E&E**: activities involving exploration for, or evaluation of, mineral resources (oil, natural gas and similar non-regenerative resources). Excludes pre-licence and post-E&E (development, extraction, refining) expenditure; mining consultants are not in scope (Ex 32); an entity with no exploration in the current period does not apply 34.11 (Ex 33).
- **Service concession arrangement** (34.12): a government or public sector body (grantor) contracts a private operator to develop (or upgrade), operate and maintain the grantor's infrastructure; the grantor controls or regulates what services, to whom and at what price, and controls any significant residual interest at the end of the term. A security-services contract with a central bank is not one (Ex 36); a hospital with unregulated prices and no grantor residual interest is the operator's own property (Q12).

## Recognition
- **Biological assets and produce** (34.3): recognise when, and only when, (a) the entity controls the asset from past events; (b) future economic benefits are probable (more likely than not — binary test, overrides Section 2 per 2.2); (c) fair value or cost is reliably measurable without undue cost or effort. Produce growing on bearer plants is recognised while it grows if fair value is reliable (Ex 13).
- **Bearer plant scope test** (34.2A): one upfront assessment at initial recognition (even if no produce yet) whether the plant can be measured separately from its produce, initially and on an ongoing basis, without undue cost or effort. Yes → plant under Section 17, produce under Section 34. No → entire plant (with produce) under Section 34.
- **E&E assets** (34.11): develop a policy under 10.4 specifying which expenditures are E&E assets; apply consistently. Exempt from 10.5 (need not consider similar requirements or Section 2). Examples (34.11A): acquisition of rights to explore; topographical, geological, geochemical, geophysical studies; exploratory drilling; trenching; sampling; evaluating technical feasibility and commercial viability. Development expenditure is **not** an E&E asset.
- **Service concessions** (34.13–34.15): the operator does **not** recognise the infrastructure as PPE or as a leased asset. It recognises a **financial asset** to the extent of an unconditional contractual right to receive cash from or at the direction of the grantor (including grantor guarantees of shortfalls), and/or an **intangible asset** to the extent of a right (licence) to charge users. A single contract can contain both (hybrid). During construction, conditional amounts due from the grantor are a contract asset under Section 23 (Ex 37).

## Measurement
### Initial
- **Policy per class, not a free choice** (34.2): fair value model for classes whose fair value is readily determinable without undue cost or effort; cost model for all others.
- **Fair value model** (34.4): fair value less costs to sell on initial recognition — costs to sell can produce an immediate loss (asset bought at CU1,000 with 5% dealer commission → recognised at CU950, CU50 loss). Costs to sell are incremental costs of the sale itself (commissions, auctioneers' fees, transfer taxes, regulatory/exchange fees). Fair value per Section 12 (34.6).
- **Cost model** (34.8): cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment. Standard does not say how to measure cost — develop a policy under 10.4/10.5 by analogy: Section 17 for assets used like PPE (dairy cows, breeding stock: purchase price plus directly attributable costs, e.g. feed and vaccines until first capable of producing milk) or Section 13 for assets used like inventory (crops, broilers: purchase, conversion costs, allocated production overheads at normal capacity). Borrowing costs never capitalised (25.2).
- **Agricultural produce** (34.5, 34.9): fair value less costs to sell at the point of harvest, under **both** models; this becomes cost for Section 13. Post-harvest processing costs are added to inventory cost under Section 13, not deducted from fair value (Ex 22, 27).
- **E&E assets** (34.11B): at cost. Depreciation of PPE used on E&E is E&E expenditure subject to the policy. Dismantling/restoration obligations under Sections 17 and 21.
- **Service concessions**: financial asset at fair value then Section 11 (34.14); intangible asset at fair value then Section 18 (34.15). Revenue for construction and operation services under Section 23 (34.16).

### Subsequent
- **Fair value model**: remeasure at each reporting date at fair value less costs to sell; changes in P&L (34.4). Section 34 is silent on subsequent costs (feed, vet, planting, weeding, irrigation, harvesting, slaughter) — policy choice to capitalise or expense.
- **Cost model**: depreciate by analogy to Section 17 from when available for use (e.g. dairy cow first capable of milking), method reflecting consumption (e.g. more depreciation in prime milking years), useful life to the entity, residual value at current prices; review on indicators. Apply Section 27 at each reporting date — the 27.1(e) scope exemption does not apply to cost-model biological assets.
- When a male calf at fair value is identified for breeding (cost-model class), its fair value less costs to sell at that date becomes its cost (Ex 25).
- **E&E assets** (34.11B–F): apply Section 17 or 18 by nature (tangible vs intangible — judgement for e.g. site information). Test for impairment only when facts and circumstances suggest carrying amount may exceed recoverable amount (34.11C), using 34.11E indicators instead of 27.7–27.10: right to explore expired/expiring and not expected to be renewed; no substantive further expenditure budgeted or planned; no commercially viable discovery and decision to discontinue; development likely but carrying amount unlikely to be fully recovered. Allocate E&E assets to CGUs per a policy (34.11F); measure, present and disclose impairment under Section 27.
- **Service concessions**: receivable at amortised cost with effective interest (Section 11); intangible amortised over period available for use by the operator (Section 18).

## Presentation
- Separate line items: biological assets at cost less accumulated depreciation and impairment (4.2(h)) and biological assets at fair value through P&L (4.2(i)). A single line is enough if all are at fair value.
- Fair value gains/losses on biological assets and gains on initial recognition of produce in P&L (34.4); the module shows them as separate lines with revenue and cost of sales.
- Sale of livestock that is not part of ordinary activities (e.g. culled dairy cows) is not revenue; in the case study, dairy cow purchases/sales are investing cash flows while beef cattle and milk are operating.
- Harvesting and slaughter costs are expenses, not costs of sale deducted from fair value (Ex 19, 22).
- E&E assets: separate class under Section 17 or 18 (34.11G).
- Service concession infrastructure is not shown as the operator's property; contract asset / receivable / intangible instead.

## Worked examples from the module
1. **Herd with births and sales (Ex 16)**: opening FV less costs to sell CU10,000; 10 heifers sold at CU200 less CU10 costs each (net CU1,900); closing CU14,000 → FV gain = 14,000 + 1,900 − 10,000 = **CU5,900**.
2. **Tomatoes at harvest (Ex 19)**: vines cost 5,500, growing costs 7,250 expensed; FV less costs to sell at year-end 24,000 → gain **18,500**. Harvest 500 kg × CU50 × 99% = **24,750** inventory; gain on produce 750; harvesting cost 1,000 expensed.
3. **Cattle slaughter (Ex 22)**: herd 10,000 → 15,000 (gain 5,000); 40% slaughtered: transfer 6,000; carcasses 10 × (700 − 2) = **6,980** (gain 980); slaughter cost 50 expensed; processing 300 → meat inventory **CU7,280**.
4. **Cost model broilers (Ex 24/27)**: 310,000 chicks × CU1 + labour 45,000 + feed/medicine 250,000 + 20% supervisor (200,000 × 45/365) + overheads 100,000 = **CU729,658** (CU2.43 per bird for 300,000). At slaughter: 300,000 × (5 − 0.5) = **1,350,000** inventory; gain **520,342** after butchering CU100,000.
5. **Dairy herd cost model (Ex 25/28)**: cows CU350, residual CU150, 5 years → depreciation CU4,000/yr for 100 cows; bulls CU400, residual 180, 2 years → CU220/yr. Male calves at FV: 60 × (90 − 7) = **CU4,980** gain. Milk 580,000 L × (0.1 − 0.01) = **CU52,200**.
6. **Service concession (Ex 40/41)**: build cost 140,000 (30% margin → 182,000); operating costs 3,236,415 (10% margin → 3,560,056); transaction price 3,742,056; EIR 7.37833%; total profit 694,548. Intangible model: intangible 182,000 amortised over 27 years = CU6,741 a year.

## Review points and common errors
- Choosing the cost model for convenience when fair value is readily available (34.2 is not a free choice; held information removes the exemption).
- Bearer plants left in Section 34 without documenting the 34.2A assessment, or produce on Section 17 bearer plants not recognised at fair value while growing (Ex 18).
- Costs to sell omitted (no day-one loss) or harvesting/slaughter costs deducted as costs to sell (Ex 19, 22).
- Agricultural produce from cost-model assets carried at cost instead of FV less costs to sell at harvest (34.9).
- Processed products (cheese, oil, lumber) measured under Section 34 rather than Section 13.
- Cost-model biological assets not tested for impairment (27.1(e) exemption does not apply).
- Missing separate SOFP lines for cost vs fair value biological assets (4.2(h)/(i)).
- E&E: development costs capitalised as E&E (34.11A); inconsistent policy; impairment indicators of 34.11E not considered; E&E not shown as a separate class (34.11G).
- Service concessions: infrastructure recognised as operator PPE; financial vs intangible split not made; borrowing costs capitalised (prohibited, Section 25).

## USD functional currency / KHR presentation currency
- **Ledger (USD)**: biological assets at fair value less costs to sell are non-monetary items measured at fair value. If fair value is determined in KHR (local market prices), translate to USD at the rate at the date fair value was determined (30.9(c)); the exchange component of the FV gain stays in P&L with the gain (30.11). Cost-model biological assets and E&E assets: historical rate (30.9(b)). Purchases and costs in KHR at spot or approximate average rates (30.7–30.8).
- Produce harvested and recognised at FV less costs to sell becomes Section 13 cost in USD at the harvest-date rate.
- Service concession receivables from the grantor denominated in KHR are monetary: retranslate at closing rate, differences in P&L (30.9(a), 30.10). Intangible concession rights are non-monetary (historical rate).
- **Translation to KHR (30.18)**: biological assets, inventory, E&E assets and concession balances at the closing rate; FV gains, harvest gains, revenue, depreciation/amortisation at transaction-date or average rates (30.18(b), 30.19); differences to the translation reserve in OCI, not recycled (30.18(c), 30.20).
- **34.7(c)(v) explicitly requires the net exchange differences arising on translation into a different presentation currency as a separate line in the biological assets reconciliation** — so the KHR roll-forward must show the translation difference, not bury it in FV gains.
- For cost-model and E&E (Section 17/18) roll-forwards, show the translation difference as an other change.
- Disclose functional currency USD, presentation currency KHR and reason (30.26).

## Differences from full IFRS Accounting Standards
- IAS 41 presumes fair value is reliably measurable (cost only where quoted prices unavailable and alternatives clearly unreliable); Section 34 uses cost whenever fair value is not readily determinable without undue cost or effort.
- All bearer plants are under IAS 16 in full IFRS; under Section 34 only those separately measurable without undue cost or effort go to Section 17.
- IAS 41 applies to biological assets related to agricultural activity; Section 34 applies to all biological assets of an entity engaged in agricultural activity.
- IFRS 6 caps E&E CGUs at operating-segment size and requires reclassification when technical feasibility and commercial viability are demonstrable; Section 34 has neither requirement.
- IFRIC 12 relies on IFRS 9, IAS 38 and IFRS 15, which differ from Sections 11, 18 and 23; IFRIC 12 measures the intangible at cost (usually no practical difference from Section 34's fair value).
- IFRIC 12 capitalises borrowing costs for the intangible model; Section 25 prohibits capitalisation.
- Disclosure: many IAS 41 disclosures (e.g. paras 40, 43, 46, 49, 50(c), 51, 54(c), 55–56) have no Section 34 equivalent; IFRS 6 paras 23–24 have no equivalent.
