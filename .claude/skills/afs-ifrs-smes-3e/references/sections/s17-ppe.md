# Section 17 — Property, Plant and Equipment (third edition)

> Sources: Educational Module 17 (third edition); IFRS for SMEs Accounting Standard (2025) paragraphs 17.1–17.33, transition A19 and A49; Section 30 paragraphs 30.7–30.11, 30.17–30.20, 30.25–30.26 for the currency section.

## When this section matters in an SME compilation
- Fixed-asset register / ledger accounts: land, buildings, machinery, motor vehicles, office and computer equipment, furniture and fittings, construction in progress, capital spares.
- Repairs and maintenance expense accounts (test for items that should be capitalised: replacement parts 17.6, major inspections 17.7) and capex accounts (test for day-to-day servicing that must be expensed, 17.15).
- Disposal / sundry income accounts (gain or loss on disposal is not revenue, 17.28) and insurance-claim receipts (17.25).
- Investment property whose fair value cannot be measured reliably without undue cost or effort (cost model under Section 17, 17.1, 17.15).
- Bearer plants (orchards, plantations) now in scope where separately measurable (17.3(a)).

## What changed in the third edition
- Bearer plants that are separately measurable from their produce without undue cost or effort, initially and on an ongoing basis, are now in scope of Section 17; the produce stays in Section 34 (17.3(a)).
- 17.21(c) clarifies that expected future reductions in the selling price of an item produced using the asset could indicate technical or commercial obsolescence.
- 17.22: a depreciation method based on revenue generated by an activity that includes the use of an asset is not appropriate.
- Fair value measurement disclosures moved to Section 12; 17.33(c) (methods and significant assumptions) is deleted; see 12.28(c) instead. Consequential amendments from revised Section 23 (date of disposal = date recipient obtains control, 17.29).
- Transition: apply amended 17.21–17.22 prospectively from date of initial application (A19). Bearer plants may be measured at fair value at the beginning of the earliest period presented as deemed cost, difference to opening retained earnings (A49). Other changes retrospective under Section 10 (A2). Effective for periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027; early application permitted.

## Scope and key definitions
- **PPE** (17.2): tangible assets (a) held for use in production or supply of goods or services, for rental to others, or for administrative purposes; and (b) expected to be used during more than one period.
- Excludes (17.3): biological assets related to agricultural activity (Section 34), except qualifying bearer plants; mineral rights and mineral reserves. Inventories (Section 13) and intangibles (Section 18) are not PPE.
- Investment property: Section 16 if fair value reliable without undue cost or effort; otherwise Section 17 cost model (17.1, 17.15).
- Module points: vehicles rented out under operating leases are PPE (only land and buildings can be investment property) (Ex 7). Guard dogs of a security firm are PPE (no agricultural activity) (Ex 8); breeding cattle are Section 34 (Ex 9). A building rented to a subsidiary is PPE in consolidated FS but investment property in the parent's separate FS (Ex 1). Land held for an undetermined purpose: Standard silent; may look to IAS 40 (investment property) via 10.6.
- **Depreciable amount**: cost (or substitute) less residual value. **Residual value**: amount currently obtainable on disposal, net of disposal costs, if the asset were already of the age and condition expected at end of useful life (current prices, not expected future proceeds). **Useful life**: period over which the asset is expected to be available for use by the entity, or units expected to be obtained; can be shorter than economic life.
- **Class**: grouping of assets of similar nature and use (e.g. land; land and buildings; machinery; motor vehicles; office equipment).

## Recognition
- Recognise cost as an asset if, and only if, future economic benefits are probable and cost is reliably measurable (17.4).
- **Spare parts, stand-by and servicing equipment** (17.5): PPE when they meet the 17.2 definition; otherwise inventory. Specialised servicing equipment used over 5 years = PPE (Ex 11); common tools used < 1 year = inventory (Ex 12); a back-up generator used irregularly = PPE (Ex 13). Materiality may justify expensing small items (e.g. spare parts and tools).
- **Replacement parts** (17.6): capitalise replacement cost if incremental future benefits; derecognise the replaced part's carrying amount (17.27–17.30) whether or not separately depreciated. If impracticable to determine, use replacement cost as indication of original cost.
- **Major inspections** (17.7): capitalise as a replacement if criteria met; derecognise any remaining carrying amount of the previous inspection. Estimated cost of a future inspection may indicate the cost of the existing inspection component.
- **Land and buildings** are separable and accounted for separately even when acquired together (17.8).
- **Day-to-day servicing** is expensed (17.15).

## Measurement
### Initial
- At cost (17.9). Cost comprises (17.10): (a) purchase price incl. legal and brokerage fees, import duties, non-refundable purchase taxes, less trade discounts and rebates; (b) directly attributable costs (site preparation, delivery and handling, installation and assembly, testing of functionality); (c) initial estimate of dismantling, removal and site restoration costs where the obligation arises on acquisition or from use other than to produce inventories.
- Expensed, not capitalised (17.11): opening a new facility; introducing a new product/service (incl. advertising); new location or new class of customer (incl. staff training); administration and general overheads; **borrowing costs** (Section 25).
- Incidental operations not necessary to bring the asset to condition: income and expenses to P&L (17.12).
- Deferred payment beyond normal credit terms: cost = present value of all future payments (17.13).
- Exchanges (17.14): fair value unless the exchange lacks commercial substance or neither fair value is reliably measurable; then carrying amount of asset given up. Standard gives no guidance on commercial substance or which fair value to use; may look to IAS 16 paras 25–26 via 10.6.
- Bearer plants on transition: optional fair value deemed cost (A49).

### Subsequent
- Policy choice per **entire class**: cost model (17.15A) or revaluation model (17.15B). Investment property under Section 17 must use the cost model (17.15).
- **Cost model** (17.15A): cost less accumulated depreciation and accumulated impairment losses.
- **Revaluation model** (17.15B): fair value at revaluation date less subsequent accumulated depreciation and impairment; only for items whose fair value can be measured reliably; revalue with sufficient regularity that carrying amount does not differ materially from fair value at reporting date; revalue the whole class (but items in a class need not be revalued simultaneously — module). Fair value per Section 12.
- **Increase** (17.15C): OCI, accumulated in revaluation surplus, except to the extent it reverses a decrease of the same asset previously in P&L (then P&L, capped at that earlier decrease).
- **Decrease** (17.15D): P&L, except to the extent of the credit balance in revaluation surplus for that asset (then OCI).
- Depreciation is still charged in a period in which the asset is revalued; after revaluation it is based on the revalued amount (Ex 28). Accumulated depreciation may be eliminated against gross amount or the gain reflected in the cost line — Section 17 does not specify (Ex 24).
- Reserve transfers (excess depreciation, or the surplus on derecognition) to retained earnings are permitted but not required; Standard does not prescribe transfers between equity components; local law may mandate or prohibit. Transfers reduce the surplus available to absorb a later decrease.
- Impairment of a revalued asset is treated as a revaluation decrease (27.6); reversal as a revaluation increase (27.30(b)). A fall below carrying amount is not an impairment if value in use exceeds carrying amount (Ex 22).

**Depreciation**
- Major components with significantly different consumption patterns are depreciated separately; others as a single asset (17.16). Applies also when a component only becomes apparent later (e.g. unexpected replacement).
- Land is not depreciated (unlimited life) except e.g. quarries and landfill sites (17.16).
- Charge to P&L unless another section requires capitalisation (e.g. manufacturing PPE into inventory, Section 13) (17.17).
- Allocate depreciable amount systematically over useful life (17.18).
- Begins when available for use (location and condition intended by management); ceases on derecognition; does **not** cease when idle or retired unless fully depreciated; usage methods may give nil charge when no production (17.20). No held-for-sale cessation (no IFRS 5 equivalent).
- Useful life factors (17.21): expected usage (capacity or output); physical wear and tear (shifts, repair programme, care while idle); technical or commercial obsolescence (incl. expected falls in selling price of output); legal or similar limits (e.g. lease expiry).
- Method reflects expected consumption pattern: straight-line, diminishing balance, units of production; revenue-based methods not appropriate (17.22). Not a free choice and need not match tax.
- **Review** residual value, useful life and method only when indicators show a significant change since the last annual reporting date (change in use, unexpected wear and tear, technological advancement, market price changes) (17.19, 17.23); account prospectively as a change in estimate (10.16).

**Impairment and compensation**
- At each reporting date apply Section 27 (17.24). Plan to dispose before the previously expected date is an impairment indicator (27.9(f), 17.26).
- Third-party compensation for impaired, lost or given-up items goes to P&L only when receivable (17.25) — gross, separate from the impairment loss (Ex 38).

**Derecognition**
- On disposal, or when no future economic benefits expected from use or disposal (17.27). Idle but expected to be used/sold = not derecognised; test for impairment (Ex 40).
- Gain or loss = net disposal proceeds less carrying amount (17.30), recognised in P&L when derecognised; gains are **not revenue** (17.28). Sale and leaseback: Section 20.
- Date of disposal = date recipient obtains control under 23.57–23.61 (17.29); retention of legal title as security only does not delay derecognition (Ex 43).

## Presentation
- PPE presented on the face of the statement of financial position; investment property presented separately (4.2; module Ex 46).
- Depreciation in P&L or capitalised into inventory / self-constructed assets (17.17).
- Revaluation movements in OCI, accumulated in revaluation surplus (17.15C–D).
- Gain on disposal presented as other income, not revenue (17.28).

## Review points and common errors
- Borrowing costs, training, opening costs, overheads and early operating losses capitalised in error (17.11; Ex 17). Refundable taxes (VAT) included in cost in error.
- Land and buildings not separated; land depreciated; building residual value inflated by land value (17.8).
- Components with different lives not separated; replaced part left in the register (17.6, 17.16).
- Depreciation started at production date rather than "available for use"; depreciation suspended while idle or after a decision to sell (17.20; Ex 30).
- Revenue-based depreciation, or tax rates used without assessing consumption pattern (17.22).
- Revaluation applied to selected items, not whole class; stale valuations; revaluation decrease taken wholly to OCI without checking the asset-specific surplus (17.15B–D).
- Insurance proceeds netted against impairment loss, or recognised before receivable (17.25).
- Gain on disposal reported in revenue (17.28).
- Spare parts and stand-by equipment wrongly held in inventory, or vice versa (17.5).
- Disclosure gaps: pledged assets and capital commitments (17.32), cost-model comparatives for revalued classes (17.33(d)), Section 12 fair value disclosures.
- Indicator-based review of useful lives not documented (17.19).

## USD functional currency / KHR presentation currency
- **Ledger (USD)**: PPE is a non-monetary item. Purchases priced in KHR or another currency are recorded at the spot rate at the transaction date (30.7); cost-model PPE is not retranslated in USD afterwards (30.9(b)). Revalued PPE valued in a foreign currency uses the rate at the date fair value was determined (30.9(c)); the exchange component follows the gain — OCI for a revaluation surplus, P&L for a P&L revaluation (30.11). Advance payments to suppliers in a foreign currency: the rate at the date of the advance fixes the cost of that portion (30.8A).
- Payables for PPE and deferred-payment liabilities denominated in KHR are monetary: retranslate at closing rate; differences in P&L (30.9(a), 30.10). They do not adjust PPE cost.
- **Translation to KHR (30.18)**: all PPE balances (cost, accumulated depreciation, carrying amount) at the closing rate at each reporting date, including comparatives (30.18(a)); depreciation, impairment, disposal gains/losses and revaluation OCI at transaction-date rates or an average rate if rates do not fluctuate significantly (30.18(b), 30.19).
- The difference arising (opening net assets at a new closing rate; flows at average vs closing) goes to OCI as a translation reserve, never recycled (30.18(c), 30.20). In the KHR roll-forward show it as a separate reconciling line ("other changes" under 17.31(e)(viii); the module's Ex 47 uses a separate exchange-difference line). Practical approach: translate opening and closing balances at respective closing rates, movements at average/transaction rates, and plug the translation difference per class — then check it is reasonable.
- The KHR revaluation surplus and translation reserve are separate components of equity; do not mix them.
- Disclose presentation currency, functional currency (USD) and reason for using KHR (30.26); exchange differences in equity (30.25(b)).
- Hyperinflation: Section 31 applies only if USD were hyperinflationary (30.21); not expected — Source silent on USD status - confirm.

## Differences from full IFRS Accounting Standards
- IAS 16 requires an **annual** review of residual value, useful life and method; Section 17 only when indicators of significant change exist.
- IAS 16 permits transfer of revaluation surplus to retained earnings on derecognition; Section 17 is silent.
- IAS 16 requires items in a class to be revalued simultaneously; Section 17 does not (only sufficient regularity).
- Full IFRS capitalises borrowing costs on qualifying assets; IFRS for SMEs expenses them.
- IAS 16 gives guidance on which fair value to use in exchanges; Section 17 does not.
- IFRS 5 stops depreciation of held-for-sale assets; no equivalent — SMEs continue to depreciate.
- Disclosure: 17.31(e)(v) and 17.32(c) have no IAS 16 equivalent; IAS 16 73(e)(viii), 74(b) and 74A have no Section 17 equivalent.
