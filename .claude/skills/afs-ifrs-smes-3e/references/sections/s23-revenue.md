# Section 23 — Revenue from Contracts with Customers (third edition)

> Sources: Educational Module 23 (third edition); IFRS Foundation webcast "Revised Section 23 — Webcast 1"; IFRS for SMEs Accounting Standard (2025) paragraphs 23.1–23.90, 23A.1–23A.37, A27–A36; Section 30 (30.7–30.10, 30.17–30.19, 30.25–30.26); Educational Module 30 Examples 15–17. All module amounts are in currency units (CU).

## When this section matters in an SME compilation
- Every sales / service income account, plus "deferred income", "customer deposits", "accrued income / unbilled revenue", "refund / rebate accruals", loyalty or voucher liabilities and returns provisions. These become **contract liabilities, contract assets, refund liabilities and returns assets** (23.77–23.81, 23.33, 23A.24).
- Bundled sales (equipment + installation / maintenance / training), long-term construction or service contracts, and contracts that are changed part-way through (Steps 2, 4 and 5; 23A.2–23A.4).
- Credit terms beyond normal terms, prompt-payment discounts, volume rebates, penalties, bonuses and rights of return (23.26–23.38, 23A.23–23A.27).
- Commission or agency income, and marketplaces or third parties in the chain (23A.14–23A.20). Licences, franchises and royalties (23A.28–23A.37).
- WIP or set-up costs of service providers, and sales commissions or bid costs (23.68–23.76).

## What changed in the third edition
- The second edition's Section 23 was based on IAS 11 and IAS 18. The third edition rewrites it on **IFRS 15** principles in simpler language, with less detail (Module, Introduction and Comparison).
- It introduces the five-step model, the Appendix to Section 23 (application guidance with equal authority, 23A.1), contract asset / contract liability presentation and an expanded set of disclosures (webcast, slides 4–5 and 16).
- Effective for annual periods beginning on or after **1 January 2027**; early application permitted. Transition (A27) is a policy choice, applied to **all** contracts: **retrospective** under Section 10 with optional exemptions (A28: completed contracts; hindsight on variable consideration; aggregate effect of pre-comparative modifications; disclose their use under A30; line-item adjustments only for the year immediately before initial application, A31), or **prospective** for contracts that begin after the date of initial application (A32). Under the prospective route, contracts in progress stay on the old Section 23, with the disclosures in A34–A36.
- Consequential amendments (A33) touch 4.11(b), 11.13, 11.14(a), 17.29, 18.1, 21.1(b), 27.1(f) and 34.16, delete 13.2(a), 13.14 and 21A.5, and add 11.7(g), 11.7A, 11.13A–B, 11.14A, 11.49(j), 11.55 and 13.2A.

## Scope and key definitions
- **Applies to** all contracts with customers **except** (23.1): leases (Section 20), insurance contracts, financial instruments and rights within Sections 9, 11, 14 and 15, and non-cash exchanges of products between entities in the same line of business to help make sales (Module Ex 2: a diesel-for-petrol swap is accounted for under Section 13).
- **Partly in scope (23.2):** if another section specifies initial measurement of part of a contract, apply it first; otherwise use Section 23. Module Ex 3: lessor's cleaning service bundled in the rent. Section 20 does not say how to split it, so Steps 2–4 of Section 23 are used.
- **Revenue** = income arising in the course of ordinary activities. **Customer** = a party that has contracted to obtain goods or services that are an output of the entity's ordinary activities, in exchange for consideration (Glossary). Proceeds from selling PP&E are not revenue (Section 17), and bond interest falls under Section 11 (Module Ex 1).
- **Contract** = an agreement that creates enforceable rights and obligations. It can be written, oral or implied by business practice (23.6). **Promise** = an obligation to transfer a distinct good or service, or a distinct bundle (23.14). The Standard says "promise" throughout, **not** "performance obligation"; principal/agent guidance uses "specified good or service" (Module notes on 23A.16).
- **Transaction price** = consideration the entity expects to be *entitled* to, excluding amounts collected for third parties such as sales tax (23.23). **Stand-alone selling price** = the price at which the entity would sell the item separately (23.43).
- **Contract asset** = a conditional right to consideration. **Trade receivable** = an unconditional right, where only the passage of time is needed (23.79–23.80). **Contract liability** = an obligation to transfer goods or services for which consideration has been received or is due (23.78).
- **Probable** = more likely than not. **Highly probable** = significantly more likely than probable (Glossary).
- **Consistency and portfolios:** apply the section consistently to similar contracts (23.4). A portfolio may be used if the result would not differ materially; quantifying the difference is not required (23.5; Module Ex 5–6).

## Recognition and measurement: the five-step model (23.3)
Steps are normally applied in sequence. For contracts that start and end within one reporting period, the model matters mainly for disclosure, but Steps 2 and 5 still determine when the contract ends (Module notes on 23.3).

### Step 1 — Identify the contract (23.6–23.13)
- Apply the model only when **all** of these are met (23.7): (a) approved, and the parties are committed; (b) each party's rights are identifiable; (c) payment terms are identifiable; (d) commercial substance (a loss-making or onerous contract can still have it); (e) collection of the consideration the entity is *entitled* to is **probable**. An expected price concession is variable consideration, not a collectability failure (Module notes).
- Reassess only on a significant change in facts (23.8). If the criteria are not met, keep reassessing (23.9). Cash received is a **liability**. Non-refundable cash becomes revenue only when the contract is complete and all or almost all consideration has been received, or when it is cancelled (23.10; Module Ex 9: a CU7,000 non-refundable advance on an unapproved contract is held as a liability).
- **Duration (23.11):** the period of present enforceable rights. Anticipated renewals are excluded (Module Ex 10).
- **Combine contracts (23.13)** entered at or near the same time with the same customer or its related parties if: negotiated as a package; the price of one depends on the other; or the goods or services form a single promise. Module Ex 11: a loss-making machine sale combined with profitable training.
- **Modifications (23.12)** are dealt with in 23A.2–23A.4 (see Application guidance).

### Step 2 — Identify the promises (23.14–23.22)
- Identify the goods or services promised, including those **implied** by practice, published policies or statements (23.16; Module Ex 14: maintenance historically given free). Set-up and administrative activities are not promises. A non-refundable upfront fee is added to the transaction price and recognised as the promises are fulfilled (23.17; Module Ex 15).
- **Distinct** = (a) the customer can benefit on its own or with readily available resources (23.18(a), 23.20). Resale value counts, the test is based on a notional customer, and it is usually met (Module Ex 17–18). And (b) the obligation is separate within the contract (23.18(b)). Indicators that it is **not separate** (23.21): integration into a combined output (Ex 19, event production); significant modification or customisation (Ex 20); high interdependence (Ex 21, design plus prototypes). A *functional* dependence is not transformative: printer and cartridges remain separate (Ex 24).
- If an item is not distinct, combine it until a distinct bundle results (23.19).
- **Series (23.15):** distinct, essentially-the-same goods or services form one promise if each would be fulfilled over time and the same progress method applies (Ex 12: monthly units; Ex 13: IT help desk = standing ready).
- Distinct items can include warranties, customer options, agency services and licences (23.22).

### Step 3 — Determine the transaction price (23.23–23.38)
- Base it on contract terms and usual practice. Assume the contract is performed as agreed and is not cancelled, renewed or modified (23.24). Sales tax collected is excluded (Ex 26: CU100 less CU10 gives CU90). An agent excludes amounts collected for the principal (Ex 27).
- Credit deterioration does **not** reduce the transaction price. It is a Section 11 impairment of the receivable or contract asset (Module notes; Ex 25).
- **Variable consideration** (discounts, rebates, refunds, penalties, bonuses; 23.26):
  1. Estimate using the **expected value** method (many similar contracts) or the **most likely amount** method (two outcomes) (23.28). Use the method consistently within a contract (23.29). Different methods are allowed for different uncertainties in the same contract (Module notes).
  2. **Constrain** the estimate: include it only to the extent it is *highly probable* the entity will become **entitled** to it (23.30). Constraint is more likely when the amount depends on third parties or weather, the resolution period is long, experience is limited, or the range of outcomes is broad (Module notes).
  3. **Update** at each reporting date. Allocate changes on the original basis and recognise the effect on fulfilled promises in the current period (cumulative catch-up). Prior periods are never restated (23.31). If uncertainty is resolved before the reporting date, use the actual amount.
- **Refund liabilities (23.33–23.35):** consideration received or receivable for goods or services transferred that is excluded from the transaction price. If nothing has been transferred yet, the amount is a **contract liability** instead (Module Table 1; Ex 40). Returns of product follow 23A.23–23A.27, not 23.34.
- **Royalties** for IP licences are excluded from the general variable-consideration model (23.32 → 23A.37).
- **Non-cash consideration (23.25, 23A.21–23A.22):** measure at fair value; if fair value cannot be reasonably estimated, use the stand-alone selling price of the goods or services given (Ex 139: 10 hours × CU500 = CU5,000; Ex 140: a car at CU1,000 in exchange for advertising space).
- **Deferred payment / financing (23.36–23.38):** if payment is **deferred** and the arrangement is a financing transaction (11.13B: beyond normal business terms, or financed at a non-market rate), discount the promised consideration at a market rate for a similar debt instrument set at contract start. The unwind is **interest revenue** under the effective interest method (11.15–11.20), presented separately from revenue from contracts with customers (23.37). It is usually shown in "other income" or "finance income" (Module notes).
  - Shortcut: use the rate that discounts the nominal amount to the cash sale price, and check that the rate is reasonable (Ex 41–42: CU10,000 due in two years discounted to CU8,264 at 10%, giving CU1,736 interest).
  - Supplier-arranged bank finance is not discounted, because the entity is not providing the finance.
  - Only **deferred** payments are discounted. **Advance** payments are not (Comparison section).
  - **23.38 practical option:** no discounting if, at contract start, payment is expected **within one year of transfer**. Expectations are not revisited, the option can be applied by portfolio (Ex 6), and it can apply to some promises in a contract but not others (Ex 43: 6- and 12-month credit qualify, 18-month credit does not; Case study 1). Disclose its use (23.89).

### Step 4 — Allocate the transaction price (23.39–23.48)
- **Not needed** if all promises are fulfilled in the period the contract starts, or at the same point in time, or if there is a single promise (23.40–23.41). Exception: a series with variable consideration still applies 23.48.
- Allocate on **relative stand-alone selling prices** determined at contract start. These are **never updated** afterwards; a modification treated as a new contract is the only reset (23.42; Ex 45).
- Best evidence is an observable price (23.43). Otherwise estimate it using a competitor's price, expected cost plus margin, or the residual method (Module notes). Methods may be combined (23.44; Ex 46: medical aid CU120 from a competitor price, fitting CU25 cost-plus, charger CU70 observable).
- **Discounts and variable consideration** are allocated proportionately unless that would not reflect the entity's expected entitlement. Then allocate to one or some promises (23.45–23.48).
  - Ex 47: CU104 for car, trailer and maintenance (SSP CU130) gives CU48 / 32 / 24.
  - Ex 48: car and trailer normally sold together for CU70, so the whole discount goes to them.
  - Ex 50: an installation bonus of CU600 goes wholly to installation.
  - Ex 51–52: per-request fees or index uplifts in a series are allocated to the day or year they relate to.

### Step 5 — Recognise revenue when (or as) a promise is fulfilled (23.49–23.67)
- A promise is fulfilled when **control** transfers: the customer has the present ability to direct use of the asset and obtain its benefits (23.49–23.50). Decide **at contract start**, for each promise, whether it is over time or at a point in time, and do not revisit (23.52; Module notes).
- **Repurchase agreements (23.51):** with a forward or call option the customer does **not** obtain control, so there is no revenue (Ex 53: sale to a bank for CU800 with a CU1,000 buy-back). Policy is developed under 10.4–10.6; the module suggests a financial liability by analogy with 11.34. A customer put / right of return does not prevent transfer.
- **Over time if any of these is met (23.54):**
  - (a) the customer simultaneously receives and consumes the benefit. Test: would another provider need to substantially re-perform the work? (Ex 55 security; Ex 56 grounds maintenance; Ex 57 an opinion fails this test.)
  - (b) the customer controls the WIP (Ex 58 integrating the customer's database; Ex 59 fails).
  - (c) the asset has no easy alternative use (23.55: significant price cut or rework cost, or substantive contractual restriction) **and** the customer must pay for work to date (23.56: an unconditional right, or an enforceable right to demand or keep payment on cancellation, covering cost plus a reasonable margin). Ex 64: a cost-plus-20% termination right meets (c). Ex 65: a right only to keep payments already received fails. Ex 60: relabelled stock can be redirected.
- **Point in time otherwise (23.57–23.59).** Indicators: present right to payment, legal title, physical possession, risks and rewards, acceptance. They are **not a checklist**.
  - Ex 66: retention of title for credit protection does not block transfer.
  - Ex 67: doors held at the SME's premises at the customer's request are controlled by the customer, so revenue is recognised before delivery.
  - Ex 68: a dealer holding stock the SME can recall means no revenue until the dealer sells.
- **Customer acceptance (23.60–23.61):** acceptance is a formality if the entity can verify the specification itself (Ex 69). If it cannot, wait for acceptance (Ex 70: integration with the customer's process).
- **Measuring progress (23.62–23.67).** Choose one method per promise that depicts transfer of control, apply it consistently, and remeasure each reporting date. A change in the measure is a **change in estimate** (Section 10, prospective; 10.18 disclosure when total inputs or outputs are re-estimated on contracts spanning more than two periods).

| Method (23.66) | Suits | Module adjustment |
|---|---|---|
| Output — surveys of work | Objective survey available | Can be costly (Ex 72 chose costs instead) |
| Output — units delivered | Each unit carries equal value | Include material WIP (Ex 74: 3.6 of 6 units = 60%, revenue CU1,440 on CU2,400) |
| Output / input — time elapsed | Even transfer or even effort | Straight line only if there is regularity (gym = standing ready) |
| Input — labour hours | Hours track transfer | Exclude rework or inefficiency (Ex 75: 40/80 h = 50%, revenue CU2,500) |
| Input — costs incurred | Common for construction | Exclude waste and **uninstalled materials** (Ex 76) |
| Right to invoice (23.67) | Billing equals value to customer (hourly rates) | No Steps 3/4 and no progress measure. Accrue unbilled work (Ex 77: 150 h × CU100 = CU15,000) |

### Measurement summary — initial and subsequent
- **Initial:** revenue = transaction price allocated to the fulfilled promise or portion. Trade receivables are recognised initially at the Section 23 amount, unless there is a financing transaction and 23.38 is not used, in which case 11.13B applies (11.13A).
- **Subsequent:** update variable consideration and refund liabilities (23.31, 23.34). Remeasure progress (23.64). Adjust refund liabilities through revenue and returns assets through expense (23.27A). Assess contract assets and receivables for impairment under Section 11 (11.7A, 11.21–11.26). Impairment is an expense, never a reduction of revenue (Module notes; Ex 94).

## Contract costs (23.68–23.76)
- **Costs to obtain** a contract (proposal time, due diligence, sales commission, marketplace fees) are **expensed when incurred**, unless another section capitalises them (for example PP&E or intangibles used to deliver a proposal) (23.68; Ex 78; Ex 137: a CU100 marketplace fee is a cost to obtain). There is no incremental-cost asset as in IFRS 15. The webcast's summary: "Expense as incurred."
- **Costs to fulfil:** apply the specific section first (Sections 13, 17, 18) (23.69). Otherwise recognise an asset only if (23.70): (a) the costs relate directly to an identifiable current or **future** contract (pre-contract costs qualify); (b) they create or improve resources used to fulfil **future** promises; (c) they are expected to be recovered. Direct labour, materials and directly related allocations qualify (23.71). G&A is expensed unless explicitly chargeable (23.72; Module notes). Costs relating to fulfilled or partly fulfilled promises are expensed (23.73).
  - Practical point: the test mainly bites for **service providers**. For over-time promises, costs after work starts usually build the *customer's* WIP and are expensed (Module notes; Table 2).
  - Ex 79: research and training time is expensed (Section 18; the employee holds the knowledge). Time on a point-in-time opinion is an asset (WIP).
  - Ex 80: moving equipment to a demolition site before work starts is an asset.
  - Ex 81: maintenance staff costs are an expense.
- **Subsequent:** cost less amortisation and impairment. Amortise on the pattern of **transfer** of the related goods or services, which may differ from the revenue pattern when variable consideration is constrained (23.74(a); Ex 83–84). Impairment follows Section 27 for *when*, but the recoverable amount = remaining **probable** consideration minus remaining costs (23.74(b)–23.76). Ex 85: CU1,800 − 180 received − 360 not probable = 1,260; less remaining costs 400 gives a recoverable amount of 860 against a carrying amount of 900, so impairment is CU40.

## SME simplifications compared with IFRS 15 (Module comparison section)
| Area | Section 23 | IFRS 15 |
|---|---|---|
| Time value of money | Discount only **deferred** payment that is a financing transaction. 23.38 one-year option | Significant financing component, whether from deferred **or advance** payments |
| Costs to obtain | Always expense (23.68) | Capitalise incremental costs expected to be recovered |
| Warranties | Separate promise only if the customer can buy without it (23A.6) | Also separates "service-type" warranties |
| Customer options | Separate only if possible without **undue cost or effort** (23A.9(b); disclose under 23.90) | No exemption |
| Right of return | Specific model based on products expected not to be returned (23A.24) | General variable-consideration model |
| Variable-consideration constraint | Framed as the amount the entity will become **entitled** to | Framed as no significant revenue reversal |
| Allocation of discount / variable consideration | Alternative method whenever the relative SSP basis does not reflect entitlement | Only in specified circumstances |
| Licences | Activities **will or could substantively affect** the customer's benefit | Activities significantly affect the IP and the customer is exposed |
| Not covered in Section 23 | Consideration payable to a customer; breakage; cases where progress cannot be reasonably measured; variable non-cash consideration and customer-contributed goods; accounting for repurchase agreements beyond the control test; **consignment and bill-and-hold**; changes in transaction price other than variable consideration | Specific requirements exist |

## Application guidance (Appendix to Section 23)
**Contract modifications (23A.2–23A.4).** A modification is an approved change in scope or price; it can be oral. Documenting what was already agreed is not a modification (Ex 108). Treatment:
1. Additional **distinct** goods at stand-alone selling price (adjusted for circumstances) → a **separate contract** (Ex 109: 4 extra properties at CU1,800 against an SSP of CU2,000, the discount reflecting saved bid costs).
2. Otherwise, if the remaining goods are distinct from those already transferred → **termination plus new contract**. The new price = unrecognised consideration + the change (Ex 110: CU250 + CU30 = CU280, allocated 210 / 70).
3. Otherwise → **cumulative catch-up** as part of the original contract (Ex 111: CU115,000 × 65% − CU70,000 = +CU4,750).

**Warranties (23A.5–23A.7).** The only test is whether the customer can buy without the warranty.
- **Optional** → separate promise. Allocate price and recognise over the warranty period; costs follow 23.69–23.76.
- **Compulsory or statutory** → not a promise. Recognise a Section 21 provision if 21.4 is met (Ex 112). Separate pricing does not change this.
- Manufacturer-fulfilled warranties give no SME provision (Ex 113). An extended warranty sold for a manufacturer is agent commission (Ex 114). A warranty-claims processing fee is a series with variable consideration (Ex 115).

**Customer options (23A.8–23A.13).** A separate promise if (a) it is a material right — available only because of this contract, and a discount to SSP — **and** (b) it can be accounted for without undue cost or effort. Judge (b) at Step 2 and do not revisit it (Ex 116–121; Ex 121: an untracked stamp card fails (b) → disclose under 23.90).
- SSP of an option = discount × likelihood of exercise, less any discount available anyway (23A.11). Do not update it later.
- Ex 122: voucher CU80 × 40% × 75% = CU24, giving goods 76.8 and voucher 19.2.
- Ex 124: loyalty points CU72,240 SSP, giving CU66,642 deferred; half redeemed → CU33,321 revenue, CU33,321 contract liability.
- Renewal options: allocate using total expected consideration including expected renewals (23A.12–23A.13). Ex 127: a CU500 joining fee + CU1,200 → CU400 to the option. The release pattern is a 10.4 policy choice.

**Principal versus agent (23A.14–23A.20).** For each **specified** good or service (a right to a future service may be the specified item, such as a ticket or meal voucher), assess control before transfer. Indicators: primary responsibility, inventory risk, pricing discretion (23A.18). Weigh them overall (Ex 135: principal despite no inventory risk).
- Principal → gross revenue. Payments to agents or subcontractors are expenses (Ex 136: CU1,800 revenue, CU1,500 cost; Ex 137: CU500 revenue, CU100 marketplace fee expensed).
- Agent → net fee as it fulfils the promise to arrange (Ex 138: gallery commission CU320 on CU800; Ex 134: voucher website).
- Not applicable when the entity sells directly to a retailer (Ex 129).

**Sales with a right of return (23A.23–23A.27)** apply when the product must be returned; a refund without return falls under 23.33 (Ex 141). Recognise revenue for products expected **not** to be returned, a **refund liability**, and a **returns asset**. The returns asset is classified as inventory at former carrying amount less recovery costs and value declines, with the credit to cost of sales. Exclude like-for-like exchanges and defective items swapped for working ones (23A.25). Use portfolios by return profile (Ex 142). Adjust each period: refund liability through **revenue**, returns asset through **expense** (23A.27; Ex 145).

**Bill-and-hold and consignment.** Section 23 contains **no specific requirements** (Comparison section). Apply the control principle and indicators (23.50, 23.58–23.59).
- Ex 67 is a bill-and-hold-type fact pattern: goods held at the customer's request, ready for delivery and not usable by the SME, so revenue is recognised before delivery.
- Ex 68 is a consignment-type pattern: no revenue until the dealer sells.
- Any further criteria (separate identification, readiness) are "Source silent - confirm".

**Licensing (23A.28–23A.37).** This applies only to the entity's **own** IP and only if the licence is distinct (Ex 146 distinct; Ex 147 not distinct → apply Step 5 to the bundle).
- **Right to access** (over time) if the entity expects activities that change the IP's substance (23A.33; Ex 148 monthly new content) or that expose the customer to positive or negative effects, such as brand support (23A.34; Ex 149 logo).
- Otherwise it is a **right to use** (point in time, not before the customer can use it; Ex 150 recipe; Ex 151 minor fixes; Ex 152 revenue on receipt of the activation code on 15 January, not the billing start or activation).
- **Sales/usage royalties** are recognised at the later of the sale or use and fulfilment of the promise the royalty is allocated to (23A.37; Ex 153: CU4,000 upfront + 3,000 × CU2 = CU6,000; Ex 154: the training share is a contract liability until training starts). Late usage reports received before authorisation are adjusting events (Section 32).

## Presentation
- When either party has performed, present a **contract asset or contract liability** for each contract **as a whole** (23.77). A contract liability is recognised when payment is made or due, whichever is earlier (23.78). With a non-cancellable contract and an invoice due in advance, present the receivable **and** contract liability **gross** (Ex 87).
- Present trade receivables **separately** from contract assets and liabilities (23.80). For example, receivables in "trade and other receivables" (4.2(b)), contract assets as their own line, contract liabilities within "trade and other payables" (4.2(l)) (Module notes).
- Alternative labels ("accrued income", "deferred income") are allowed, but the balance must contain only customer-contract amounts, and contract assets must be distinguishable from receivables (23.81).
- Refund liabilities are a separate liability from contract liabilities (Module Table 1). Returns assets are classified as inventory (23A.24(c)); they may be described differently (4.9(b)).
- Interest revenue from financing is presented **separately** from revenue from contracts with customers (23.37).

## Worked examples from the module
1. **Variable consideration penalties (Ex 29, 33, 37, 40).** A CU40,000 refurbishment with a CU5,000/week penalty. Expected penalty CU6,000 gives a gross estimate of CU34,000, **constrained to CU30,000**. The full CU40,000 paid upfront is a contract liability. At year-end the transaction price is CU28,000 and the work is 60% done: revenue CU16,800, refund liability CU7,200, contract liability CU16,000. Finished 3 weeks late: entitlement CU25,000, so next-year revenue is CU8,200, with no restatement.
2. **Uninstalled materials (Ex 76).** Price CU26,000, expected cost CU20,000 including CU6,000 of appliances. Incurred CU18,000 including CU4,000 of appliances not yet installed. Progress = 14,000/20,000 = 70%. Revenue = 70% × (26,000 − 4,000) + 4,000 = **CU19,400**.
3. **Financing plus partial 23.38 option (Case study 1).** Machine, installation and 12-month maintenance for CU200,000 due in 12 months; 12% market rate. Machine and installation (SSP CU140,000) are discounted to CU125,000. Maintenance (CU60,000) is not discounted (23.38). Transaction price CU185,000. The CU15,000 discount is allocated only to the machine (CU102,679) and installation (CU22,321). 20X1: revenue CU155,000 (125,000 + 30,000), interest CU7,289. 20X2: revenue CU30,000, interest CU7,711.
4. **Returns (Ex 144).** 6,400 units at CU80 (cost CU40), 30% return rate, 320 already returned. Revenue CU358,400. Refund liability 1,600 × 80 = CU128,000. Returns asset = 64,000 − 4,000 shipping − 3,840 defect write-down = **CU56,160**.
5. **Modification and lost bonus (Case study 2).** Yacht, cost-to-cost method, CU2,000,000 + CU250,000 bonus (highly probable at start). The swim-platform modification (+CU600,000, costs +CU500,000) is not distinct: catch-up = 750,000 − 2,850,000 × 500/2,000 = **−CU37,500**. In 20X4 the bonus is no longer highly probable; the transaction price drops to CU2,600,000 and 20X4 revenue is CU298,750, a loss year.

## Review points and common errors
- Revenue is reduced for bad debts. This is wrong: impairment is a Section 11 expense (Ex 25, 94).
- Sales tax or principal's money is included in revenue (23.23). Gross vs net is decided without the specified-good control analysis.
- Sales commissions or bid costs are capitalised. Section 23 always expenses them (23.68).
- Statutory or compulsory warranties are deferred as revenue instead of provided for under Section 21 (23A.7). Optional extended warranties are recognised upfront.
- Points, vouchers and renewal discounts are ignored with no 23.90 disclosure. Option SSPs are updated after inception.
- Variable consideration is not constrained, or prior years are restated when estimates change (23.31 is catch-up in the current period).
- Advances are discounted (only deferred payments are), or a financing component is missed on credit beyond normal terms longer than one year.
- Inconsistent progress methods for similar promises. Uninstalled materials or rework are left in the cost-to-cost measure. Customer-controlled WIP is still carried as inventory.
- Contract asset and receivable are netted, or a receivable and contract liability are netted. The opening and closing balances and the 23.84(b) figure are not disclosed.
- Refund liability and returns asset are netted, or the returns asset is credited to revenue (23A.24, 23A.27).
- The over-time vs point-in-time conclusion is revisited mid-contract (23.52). Legal title is treated as decisive (Ex 66).
- Transition: mixing retrospective and prospective by contract (A27 prohibits it); A30 and A36 disclosures omitted.

## USD functional currency / KHR presentation currency
**Step A — in the USD ledger (KHR-denominated contracts are foreign currency transactions, 30.6):**
- **Revenue** is recorded at the USD/KHR spot rate on the date of the transaction (30.7), meaning when it first qualifies for recognition, typically when control transfers (30.8). A weekly or monthly average is acceptable unless rates fluctuate significantly (30.8).
- **Advance consideration in KHR** (deposits, upfront fees, prepaid service). The contract liability is a **non-monetary** item, recorded at the rate on the date of receipt (or due date, 23.78) and **not retranslated** at year-end (30.8A, 30.9(b)). When the promise is fulfilled, the portion of revenue covered by the advance uses the **advance-date rate**; the remainder uses the rate at recognition. Each advance has its own transaction date (30.8A). Module 30 Ex 17: FCU200 advance at 1.15 = LCU300 is released at LCU300; the balance FCU250 at 1.7 = LCU425 is a monetary receivable; retranslation at 1.9 gives an FX gain of LCU50 in P&L.
- **KHR trade receivables** are monetary and retranslated at the closing rate, with differences in **profit or loss** (30.9(a), 30.10). They are not revenue adjustments.
- **Refund liabilities** in KHR cash appear to be monetary under the Glossary definition (a fixed or determinable number of currency units). Credit-note refunds redeemable in goods: Source silent - confirm.
- **Contract assets** in KHR: monetary vs non-monetary classification is Source silent - confirm.
- **Returns assets** (inventory) and **contract fulfilment assets** are non-monetary at historical cost (30.9(b)).
- **Financing component on a KHR deferred receivable:** 23.36 requires a market rate "for a similar debt instrument"; whether that means a KHR rate is not spelled out (Source silent - confirm). The resulting receivable is monetary (30.9(a)).
- Variable-consideration updates on KHR contracts: rate for catch-up adjustments is Source silent - confirm; apply 30.7–30.8 at the date of the change.

**Step B — translating USD statements into KHR (30.17–30.19; USD is not hyperinflationary, so 30.21 / Section 31 does not apply):**
- **Revenue, interest revenue, cost of sales and contract-cost amortisation** are translated at transaction-date rates, or the **average rate for the period** if rates do not fluctuate significantly (30.18(b), 30.19). For large one-off point-in-time sales or catch-up adjustments, consider the actual date rate if the average is not representative (30.19).
- **All contract balances** — receivables, contract assets, contract liabilities (even the non-monetary ones held at historical USD), refund liabilities, returns assets and fulfilment assets — are translated at the **closing USD/KHR rate** at each reporting date, including comparatives (30.18(a)). The "no retranslation" rule in 30.9(b) operates only in the functional currency.
- Differences from average-rate P&L vs closing-rate balances, and from opening net assets, go to **OCI / translation reserve** and are never recycled (30.18(c), 30.20). Disclose under 30.25(b).
- **23.83–23.84 roll-forwards in KHR:** opening balances are at the prior closing rate, closing balances at the current rate, and the 23.84(b) revenue amount at transaction or average rates. How to present the translation difference in the reconciliation is Source silent - confirm; a separate "translation difference" line keeps the reconciliation arithmetically complete.
- Disclose KHR as the presentation currency, USD as the functional currency, and the reason (30.26).

## Differences from full IFRS Accounting Standards
See the simplifications table above (Module comparison section). In summary: the same principles in simpler language. SMEs say "promise", not "performance obligation". There is no significant financing component for advances. Costs to obtain are always expensed. The warranty test is optionality only. There is an undue cost or effort exemption for options, a bespoke right-of-return model and a modified licence test. IFRS 15 guidance on consideration payable to customers, breakage, progress that cannot be measured, repurchase accounting, and **consignment and bill-and-hold** is absent. Disclosures are much lighter: Section 23 has no equivalent of IFRS 15 paragraphs 110–111, 113(a), 115, 116(c), 117–118, 120–122, 123(a), 124(b), 125–126 and 128(a) or 129. Mapped equivalents are 113(b)→23.83(b), 114→23.82, 116(a)→23.83(a)/23.84(a), 116(b)→23.84(b), 119→23.85, 123(b)→23.87, 124(a)→23.86, 127→23.88 and 128(b)→23.89.
