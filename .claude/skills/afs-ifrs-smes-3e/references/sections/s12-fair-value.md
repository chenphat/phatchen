# Section 12 — Fair Value Measurement (third edition)

> Sources: Educational Module 12 (third edition); IFRS for SMEs Accounting Standard (2025) paragraphs 12.1–12.32 and Appendix A paragraphs A12–A14, with cross-references to 2.89, 16.7–16.8, 17.15B–17.15D and 30.9–30.20.

## When this section matters in an SME compilation
- **Investment property** measured at fair value through P&L (16.7). This is the most common SME case.
- **PPE** under the revaluation model (17.15B). Land and buildings are valued separately (17.8).
- **Equity investments** in non-convertible preference shares and non-puttable ordinary or preference shares (11.14(c)). Also Part II financial instruments at fair value.
- **Biological assets** and agricultural produce at fair value less costs to sell (Section 34).
- Other triggers:
  - Business combinations (Section 19 acquisition-date fair values).
  - Impairment tests using fair value less costs of disposal (27.14).
  - Non-cash distributions to owners (Section 22).
  - Retained interest on loss of control (Section 9).
  - The compound-instrument split (Section 22).

## What changed in the third edition
- **Section 12 is entirely new.**
  - In the 2nd edition, fair value guidance sat in Section 11 (old 11.27–11.32, now deleted). Other sections either cross-referred to it or had their own guidance, and fair value disclosures were spread across sections.
  - The 3rd edition puts measurement and disclosure into a **single framework** that applies to all fair value measurements unless exempted (Module 12, "What has changed").
- The definition of fair value is clarified as an **exit price** (12.3–12.4; 2.89). It is based on IFRS 13 definitions in simplified language.
- The **fair value hierarchy (Levels 1–3)** is now explicit and general, and applies to non-financial items too. The 2nd edition's financial-instrument "order of precedence" was the forerunner: quoted price, then binding sale agreement, then valuation technique (module notes to 12.22).
- New, more explicit **disclosures** (12.28–12.32).
- Section 12 **does not** add new fair value requirements, change what is measured at fair value, or prescribe presentation of fair value changes (module).
- **Transition (A12–A14):**
  - Apply Section 12 and 2.89 **prospectively** from the date of initial application. For a 31 December 2027 year-end, that date is 1 January 2027.
  - No restatement of comparative fair values, and no Section 12 disclosures for comparatives before that date (A13).
  - Consequential amendments: 11.14(c), 11.43, 11.57, 13.3(a), 14.10, 14.15, 15.15, 15.21, 16.7, 17.15B, 27.9(c), 27.14, 28.15(b), 34.4, 34.6. Deletions: 11.27–11.32, 12.12 (old), 16.10(a), 17.33(c), 34.7(b). Additions: 26.1D–26.1E. These amendments are also prospective.
  - Effective for periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027, with early application permitted.

## Scope and key definitions
**Scope (12.1–12.2):**
- Section 12 applies whenever another section requires or permits fair value measurement or disclosure.
- Wholly excluded from Section 12: share-based payments (Section 26, which uses its own 26.1E definition under 26.1D) and leases (Section 20).
- Measured under Section 12, but **12 disclosures not required**: defined benefit plan assets (Section 28) and assets whose recoverable amount is fair value less costs of disposal (Section 27).
- **Not fair value, so outside Section 12:** value in use (Section 27); provisions at best estimate (Section 21, Ex 4); DBO (Ex 5); selling price less costs to complete and sell for inventory. The module's text says "Section 2 Inventories", which looks like a typo for Section 13.

**Definitions (Glossary, as reproduced in the module):**
- **Fair value:** "the price that would be received to sell an asset, or paid to transfer a liability, in an orderly transaction between market participants at the measurement date" (12.4). It is market-based, not entity-specific. The entity's intention to hold or settle is irrelevant.
- **Orderly transaction:** usual and customary market exposure before the measurement date. Not a forced liquidation or distress sale.
- **Market participants:** independent (not related parties under Section 33), knowledgeable, able to transact, and willing (motivated but not forced). You need not identify specific participants.
- **Principal market:** the market with the greatest volume and level of activity for the asset or liability.
- **Most advantageous market:** maximises net receipts (or minimises net payments) after transaction **and** transport costs.
- **Transaction costs:** directly attributable to disposal, essential to it, and not incurred without the decision to sell.
- **Highest and best use:** the market participants' use that maximises value of the asset, or of the group in which it is used.
- **Active market:** transactions with sufficient frequency and volume to give ongoing pricing information.
- **Observable inputs:** developed from market data. **Unobservable inputs:** best information about market participants' assumptions where no market data exists.

## Recognition
Section 12 contains no recognition criteria. Recognition comes from the section that requires fair value (12.1; module notes). Step 1 is to identify the asset or liability and its unit of account, which may be a single asset or a group such as a CGU.

## Measurement
### Initial
**Four-step approach (module, Introduction):**
1. Identify the asset or liability (12.1–12.2).
2. Find the **principal market**, or failing that the **most advantageous market** (12.6–12.9).
   - The entity must have **access** to the market at the measurement date (12.7).
   - Presume the market the entity normally uses, absent contrary evidence. No exhaustive search is needed.
   - If there is no observable market, still assume a transaction at the measurement date.
3. For **non-financial assets**, decide on highest and best use: stand-alone or in combination (12.10–12.13).
4. Choose valuation technique(s) that maximise relevant observable inputs (12.14–12.15).

**Characteristics (12.5):**
- Include condition, location and restrictions **if market participants would price them**.
- A restriction that is a characteristic of the asset counts. Examples: an easement (Ex 10); a legal ban on sale until remediation (Ex 8).
- An **entity-specific** restriction does not count. Examples: a property pledged as loan security (Ex 9); a donor's use restriction that does not pass to a buyer (Ex 10).

**Costs (12.8–12.9):**
- **Ignore transaction costs** in the price. They are used only to identify the most advantageous market.
- **Deduct transport costs** where location is a characteristic.

**Highest and best use (12.10–12.13):**
- The use must be physically possible, legally allowed and financially feasible (12.11).
- **Current use is presumed** unless market or other factors suggest otherwise (12.12).
- If in-combination use gives maximum value, assume market participants hold the complementary assets and liabilities, and make consistent assumptions across the group (12.13).
- Liabilities included in the group are working-capital liabilities, not general financing (module notes).
- Highest and best use does **not** apply to financial assets or liabilities.

**Valuation approaches (12.14–12.15)** have no hierarchy among themselves (module notes):

| Approach | What it does | Typical SME use (module) |
|---|---|---|
| Market | Prices or multiples from transactions in identical or similar items | Comparable property sales; price per m²; P/E multiples; sector benchmarks (per hectare, per kg) |
| Cost | Current replacement cost of service capacity, adjusted for obsolescence | Customised installed plant (Ex 19; Case study 2) |
| Income | Converts future amounts to a single discounted amount: present value techniques, option pricing, multi-period excess earnings | Unlisted preference shares (Ex 20); biological assets (Ex 28, Ex 30) |

**Present value techniques for fair value:**
- Use market-participant cash flows and discount rates, **not** entity-specific ones. This differs from value in use (module).
- Do not double count risk between the cash flows and the rate.
- Be consistent: nominal with nominal, real with real, pre-tax with pre-tax, post-tax with post-tax.
- The discount rate must be consistent with the **currency of the cash flows**.

**Multiple techniques:** assess how reasonable the range is, and pick the point in it most representative of fair value. Weighting may be better than a simple average (module).

**Bid–ask (12.17):**
- Use the price within the spread most representative of fair value.
- Bid for assets and ask for liabilities is permitted but not required. Mid-market is allowed as a practical expedient.
- The spread is relevant only to financial instruments in intermediated markets.

### Subsequent
- **Change in technique or its application (12.16):** treat as a change in accounting estimate (10.17), applied prospectively in the period of change. The Section 10 estimate-change disclosures are **not** required.
  - A change is appropriate when the new technique is equally or more representative. Examples: new markets, new information, better techniques, changed conditions.
- **Reliable measure (12.18–12.20):**
  - A technique is reliable if it reasonably reflects market pricing and its inputs reflect market expectations of risk and return.
  - For unquoted assets, reliability exists if the variability in the range of reasonable fair values is not significant, or the probabilities within the range can be assessed.
  - Fair value of an asset acquired from an outside party is normally estimable.
- **Losing reliability, or undue cost or effort becoming available (12.21):** the carrying amount at the last reliable date becomes **new cost**. Then measure at cost less impairment until a reliable fair value (or one available without undue cost or effort) returns. On return, follow the relevant section's rules for fair value changes (module notes).
- Undue cost or effort is available **only** where the relevant section grants it (2.28–2.31). It cannot be applied by analogy.
- Choosing the cost model instead of PPE revaluation is a **policy choice**, not use of the exemption (module notes).

## Presentation
Section 12 does not prescribe presentation of fair value changes. Follow the measuring section:
- Investment property gains and losses go to P&L (16.7).
- PPE revaluation increases go to OCI and the revaluation surplus, except reversals of earlier P&L decreases (17.15C). Decreases go to P&L after any surplus for that asset is used (17.15D).

The quantitative disclosures go **in a table** unless another format is more useful (12.32).

## Worked examples from the module
1. **Transaction vs transport costs (Ex 12).**
   - Market A: price CU26, transaction costs CU3, transport CU2, net CU21.
   - Market B: price CU25, transaction costs CU1, transport CU2, net CU22.
   - If A is principal: fair value = 26 − 2 = **CU24**.
   - If neither is principal, B is most advantageous: fair value = 25 − 2 = **CU23**.
   - Case study 1 (oranges): Brazil is principal, so CU15 − 9 = **CU6**. With no principal market, India gives the best net amount but there is no access, so Argentina (net 5 vs 3) applies: CU12 − 5 = **CU7**.
2. **Biological asset (Ex 17).** 200 ha × 70 t = 14,000 t. Per tonne: CU15 − harvest CU3 − transport CU1 = CU11. Fair value = **CU154,000**.
3. **Unlisted shares, market approach (Ex 18 / Ex 21).**
   - P/E method: CU70,000 maintainable profit × P/E 15 × 0.8 (20% lack-of-marketability discount) = CU840,000 for the company; ÷ 5,000 shares = CU168 per share; × 250 shares = **CU42,000**.
   - Net-asset method: CU850,000 ÷ 5,000 = CU170 per share; × 250 = **CU42,500**.
4. **Cost approach (Ex 19).** Customised machine cost CU10,000. Price index +15% gives replacement cost CU11,500. Obsolescence factor 0.8 gives **CU9,200**. These are Level 3 inputs.
5. **Disclosure table (Ex 29).**
   - Equity instruments CU50,000: A Ltd CU20,000 at Level 1; B Ltd CU30,000 at Level 3 (DCF, Level 3 gain CU2,000).
   - Investment property CU350,000: Area A CU200,000 at Level 2; Area B CU150,000 at Level 3 (Level 3 gain CU30,000).
   - Inputs disclosed with ranges and weighted averages: WACC 7%–16% (12.1%); EBITDA multiple 10–13 (11.3).

## Review points and common errors
- Deducting **transaction costs** from fair value (only transport costs are deducted). Or forgetting transport costs where location matters (12.8–12.9).
- Using the entity's busiest market rather than the market-wide principal market. Or choosing a market the entity cannot access (Ex 11; Case study 1).
- Reflecting **entity-specific** restrictions (loan pledges, owner intentions, below-market leases to a charity) as though they were characteristics of the asset (Ex 7, Ex 9).
- Using entity-specific discount rates or cash flows (value-in-use thinking) in a fair value DCF (Ex 20).
- Calling a measurement Level 2 when a significant unobservable adjustment was made (12.26). Calling anything other than unadjusted quoted prices Level 1. Letting costs to sell affect the level (module notes to 12.22).
- Taking undue cost or effort where no exemption is offered, or by analogy. Not re-assessing it each period for recurring measurements. Leaving out the 2.31 disclosure.
- Restating comparatives on transition, or giving Section 12 disclosures for pre-transition comparatives. Neither is required (A12–A13).
- Disclosing 12.29 for non-recurring or Level 1/2 items. 12.29 covers **recurring Level 3 only**.
- Classes that do not reconcile to SFP line items (12.31).
- Accepting an external valuer's report without checking that it complies with Section 12 (module: the SME remains responsible).
- **Paragraph cross-reference in the module:** Ex 22 cites "16.8" for recognising fair value changes in P&L. In the 2025 Standard that requirement is **16.7**; 16.8 deals with loss of a reliable measure. Cite 16.7.

## USD functional currency / KHR presentation currency
- **Measure fair value in USD**, the functional currency. Apply all Section 12 steps (market, highest and best use, technique, level) and book the result in the USD ledger.
- **Translate at the closing rate.** In the KHR statements, all assets and liabilities at fair value (like all other balances) are translated at the **closing rate** at each SFP date, including comparatives (30.18(a)).
- **Fair value gains and losses** recognised in P&L or OCI are income and expense items. Translate them at transaction-date rates or an appropriate average rate (30.18(b), 30.19). The difference against closing-rate balances goes to the **translation reserve in OCI**, which is never reclassified (30.18(c), 30.20).
- **Prices or valuations quoted in KHR or another non-USD currency.** For a USD-functional entity, a non-monetary item at fair value determined in a foreign currency is translated into USD at the rate **at the date fair value was determined** (30.9(c)). The exchange component of the gain or loss follows the gain: P&L if the gain is in P&L, OCI if in OCI (30.11). Then translate to KHR at the closing rate (30.18).
- **Present value inputs.** The discount rate must suit the currency of the cash flows (module, present value principles). Discount USD cash flows at USD-based rates, and do not mix KHR rates with USD cash flows.
- **Disclosures (12.28–12.31) in KHR.**
  - Carrying amounts at the closing rate.
  - 12.29 Level 3 gains and losses at the rates used for the P&L/OCI line items, so they agree to the KHR statement of comprehensive income.
  - Class subtotals reconciled to the KHR SFP lines (12.31).
- **Hierarchy level.** Translation into KHR does not change the level. The level depends on the inputs to the USD valuation. (This is an inference from 12.22 and 30.18; the source does not state it expressly.)
- Also disclose the presentation currency, the USD functional currency and the reason for the difference (30.26), plus the exchange-difference amounts (30.25).

## Differences from full IFRS Accounting Standards
- IFRS 13 has specific guidance that Section 12 omits as unlikely to be relevant to SMEs:
  - fair value of **liabilities and own equity instruments**, including non-performance risk;
  - financial assets and liabilities with **offsetting positions** in market or counterparty credit risk.
- Section 12 is written in simpler language.
- **Disclosures:** Section 12 keeps the equivalents of IFRS 13.93(a)–(c), 93(e)(i)–(ii), 94 and 99. There is no equivalent of IFRS 13.91, 92, 93(e)(iii)–(iv), (f), (g), (h), (i) or 95–98. The module's table also lists 93(c) as having no equivalent, which conflicts with its 93(c) to 12.28(c) mapping. Source ambiguous - confirm.
