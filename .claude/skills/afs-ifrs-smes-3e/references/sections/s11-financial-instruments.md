# Section 11 — Financial Instruments (third edition)

> Sources: Educational Module 11 (third edition); IFRS for SMEs Accounting Standard (2025) paragraphs 11.1–11.75 and transition A11; Section 4 paragraph 4.2 for presentation; Section 30 paragraphs 30.7–30.13, 30.17–30.21, 30.25–30.26 for the currency section.

## When this section matters in an SME compilation
- Cash and bank (current, fixed-term and notice deposits), trade receivables, staff/related-party loans, trade and other payables, accruals payable in cash (rent, interest), bank loans and overdrafts — almost every SME has Part I instruments (11.1, 11.5).
- Investments in shares (listed or unlisted), bonds, mutual funds; intercompany loans; dividends receivable (11.8(d), 11.14A).
- Anything with a derivative flavour: FX forwards, interest rate swaps, commodity contracts that can be net settled, oil/index-linked loans, convertible instruments, issued financial guarantees — these push the item into Part II at fair value (11.6, 11.11). Every entity must check Part II scope to show it is exempt (11.1).
- Foreign-currency (e.g. KHR) receivables, payables, bank balances — monetary items remeasured through profit or loss (11.10(b), 30.10).
- Factoring, sale-and-repurchase, loan restructurings/modifications (derecognition, 11.33–11.38).

## What changed in the third edition
- Former Section 11 (Basic Financial Instruments) and Section 12 (Other Financial Instrument Issues) merged into one **Section 11 Financial Instruments**: Part I = basic instruments (old s11), Part II = "Other Financial Instrument Issues" (old s12). The new Section 12 is now Fair Value Measurement.
- **IAS 39 option removed** — all SMEs apply Section 11 recognition and measurement.
- Financial guarantee contracts: issued at nil consideration where the specified debtor is another group entity → Section 21 (21.1A); all other issued financial guarantees → Part II (11.6(g), 11.49(k)).
- 11.9(b) clarified: a party "may pay or receive reasonable compensation on early termination" and still meet the condition — i.e. prepayment features with **negative compensation** can still qualify for amortised cost.
- **New supplementary principle 11.9ZA**: a debt instrument failing 11.9(a)–(d) is still basic (Part I) if its contractual cash flows are **solely payments of principal and interest** (SPPI) on the outstanding principal on specified dates. This is the "principle-based" fallback; not a business-model test.
- **11.11A**: no reassessment of classification after initial recognition.
- 11.13–11.13A amended for revised Section 23: trade receivables initially at the Section 23 amount.
- Dividend recognition moved from Section 23 into 11.14A / 11.55.
- Fair value guidance and fair value disclosures moved to the new Section 12 (11.27–11.32 deleted; 11.57 cross-refers).
- **New disclosures**: age analysis of trade receivables and other amortised-cost financial assets (11.43) and maturity analysis of financial liabilities (11.43A), time bands per 11.43B.
- 11.49(g) amended for revised Section 19 (contingent consideration).
- **Impairment model unchanged — still incurred loss** (objective evidence of a loss event, 11.21–11.22). The module's IFRS comparison confirms Section 11 uses "incurred losses", not expected credit losses.
- Transition (A11): effective for periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027, early application permitted; retrospective under Section 10. Entities previously on IAS 39: do not re-recognise items derecognised under IAS 39 (may derecognise at date of initial application, or keep until settled, items IAS 39 had not derecognised); assess 11.9–11.9ZA on facts at date of initial application and apply that classification retrospectively; if retrospective effective interest is impracticable use fair value as gross carrying amount/amortised cost; hedge accounting prospective.

## Scope and key definitions
- **Financial instrument**: a contract giving rise to a financial asset of one entity and a financial liability or equity instrument of another (11.3). Financial asset = cash, another entity's equity instrument, contractual right to receive cash/financial asset or to exchange on potentially favourable terms, or certain own-equity-settled contracts (Glossary). Financial liability = contractual obligation to deliver cash/financial asset or exchange on potentially unfavourable terms, or certain own-equity-settled contracts.
- Statutory items are not financial instruments: current tax and deferred tax are "statutory not contractual"; warranty obligations (repair/replace) are not financial liabilities (Case Study 1).
- **Part I exclusions (11.7)**: subsidiaries/associates/joint arrangements under Sections 9/14/15; own equity (Section 22); leases (Section 20) — but lessor lease receivables and lessee lease payables use 11.33–11.38 derecognition, and lessor lease receivables use 11.21–11.26 impairment; employee benefit plans (Section 28); share-based payment (Section 26); reimbursement assets (Section 21); Section 23 rights/obligations except trade receivables meeting 11.8(b). 11.7A: impairment rules 11.21–11.26 also apply to **contract assets**.
- **Basic instruments (11.8)**: (a) cash; (b) a debt instrument meeting 11.9 **or** 11.9ZA; (c) a loan commitment that cannot be net settled in cash and will meet 11.9 when executed; (d) investments in non-convertible preference shares and non-puttable ordinary or preference shares.
- **11.9 conditions (all of (a)–(d))**:
  - (a) returns, assessed in the currency of denomination, are a fixed amount; a fixed rate; a variable rate equal to a single quoted/observable interest rate (e.g. SONIA); or a combination of fixed and variable where both are positive. Interest = rate × principal outstanding.
  - (b) no contractual provision that could cause the holder to lose principal or current/prior-period interest (subordination is not such a provision; reasonable compensation on early termination allowed).
  - (c) prepayment/put features not contingent on future events, except protecting the holder against credit deterioration/change of control, or either party against tax/law changes.
  - (d) no conditional returns or repayment provisions other than (a) variable rate and (c) prepayment.
- **11.9ZA principle**: SPPI on specified dates; exposure to unrelated risks or volatility (equity prices, commodity prices) is unlikely to pass. "Interest" = reasonable compensation for time value of money, credit risk, other basic lending risks and costs (liquidity risk, administrative costs, profit margin).
- Module guidance on 11.9: an index other than an interest rate (price index, commodity index, government inflation rate) fails 11.9(a). Profit-linked cash flows fail 11.9(b). A significant risk of debtor non-payment does **not** fail 11.9(b) (not a contractual provision). Prepayment amount must be substantially equal to unpaid principal and interest; prepayment triggered by contingent events (IPO, business combination, 50% fall in gold price, retirement of a major shareholder, tax/legislation change) fails 11.9(c). Basic caps, collars and floors do not by themselves fail 11.9(a).
- **Examples that normally qualify** (11.9A, 11.9B, 11.10): fixed-then-variable bank loan; SONIA + 200bp; loan with early termination penalty; trade accounts and notes; bank loans; **accounts payable in a foreign currency** (FX changes to P&L under 30.10); intra-group loans due on demand; debt immediately repayable on default.
- **Examples that fail → Part II (11.6, 11.11)**: equity investments other than 11.8(d) shares (e.g. convertible preference shares); swaps and forwards that can be cash settled; options; convertible debt; asset-backed securities, repos, securitised receivables; designated hedging instruments; commitments to make a loan; net-settleable commitments to receive a loan; acquirer's contingent consideration; issued financial guarantees (except nil-consideration intra-group). Module: oil-indexed debt (Ex 33), note repayable in government bonds (Ex 32) and a mutual fund holding (Case Study 1) are Part II.
- **Part II scope (11.49–11.51)** excludes Part I items, Sections 9/14/15 investments, employee benefits, most insurance contracts, own equity, Section 20 leases (but leases with loss-causing terms unrelated to asset price, FX, variable market rates or default are Part II), share-based payment, reimbursement assets, Section 23 items except trade receivables not under Part I, nil-consideration intra-group guarantees. Contracts to buy/sell non-financial items are Part II if they impose non-typical risks (11.50) or can be net settled — unless held for own use (11.51).
- Module: lease payments in FCU are Section 20 (FX is a "related" risk, Ex 114); export contract priced in the seller's currency is outside Part II (Ex 115); copper purchase indexed to PPI is Part II (Ex 116), indexed to copper price is not (Ex 117).

## Recognition
- Recognise a financial asset or liability only when the entity becomes party to the contractual provisions (11.12, 11.52). A forward contract is recognised at inception even at nil fair value (Ex 124).
- Firm commitments to buy/sell goods are generally not recognised until one party performs (module, Part I notes).
- Dividends receivable: recognise in P&L only when the right to receive is established, inflow probable and amount reliable (11.14A, 11.55). Module Ex 65–66: dividend proposed 18 Dec 20X4, approved 28 Feb 20X5 — a December year-end investor does not recognise it in 20X4; a March year-end investor recognises CU20,000 at 31 March 20X5.
- **No reclassification** after initial recognition (11.11A). If terms change (Ex 39: SONIA loan later indexed to oil), test for substantial modification under 11.37 instead.

## Measurement
### Initial
| Instrument | Initial measurement | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Part I, general | Transaction price **including** transaction costs (unless subsequently at FVTPL) | 11.13 |
| Trade receivable | Amount determined under Section 23 (normally invoice price) | 11.13A |
| Trade receivable that is in effect a financing and 23.38 practical expedient not applied | PV of future receipts at market rate for a similar debt instrument | 11.13A, 11.13B |
| Financing transaction (payment deferred beyond normal terms, or below-market / interest-free rate) | PV of future payments at a market rate for a similar debt instrument at initial recognition | 11.13B |
| Short-term payable on normal credit | Undiscounted amount owed (invoice) | 11.13 examples |
| Liability repayable on demand | Full face amount, not discounted | module, Ex 54 |
| Part I shares subsequently at FVTPL | Price paid; transaction costs to P&L | 11.13, Ex 42 |
| Part II instruments | Fair value, normally the transaction price; transaction costs excluded (12.8) | 11.53, Ex 122 |

- 23.38 option (per the module): an entity may not discount if it expects the customer to pay within one year of transfer; an entity not applying it (by choice or ineligibility) uses 11.13B for financing arrangements.
- Market rate for a "similar debt instrument" reflects currency, term, borrower credit rating, rate the borrower would normally pay, other factors. For arm's-length financing, the cash sale price normally approximates the PV.
- **Transaction costs**: incremental costs directly attributable to acquisition/issue/disposal (fees and commissions to agents, advisers, brokers; regulatory levies; transfer taxes and duties). Not debt premiums/discounts, financing costs or internal administrative costs. Added to financial assets / deducted from financial liabilities at amortised cost or cost; expensed for FVTPL items; costs of a future disposal never included; costs of an abandoned transaction expensed.
- Below-market loans: the day-one difference is not a financial instrument — employee loan difference is employee benefit under Section 28 (Ex 49); customer loan difference is an expense unless it meets the Section 18 intangible definition (Ex 50). Parent-to-subsidiary low-rate loan is not at market rate (module notes).

### Subsequent
| Category | Subsequent measurement | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Debt instruments meeting 11.8(b) | Amortised cost, effective interest method | 11.14(a) |
| Current debt instruments (incl. current trade receivables/payables) not financing | Undiscounted cash expected to be received/paid, net of impairment | 11.14(a) |
| Loan commitments under 11.8(c) | Cost (sometimes nil) less impairment | 11.14(b) |
| 11.8(d) shares publicly traded or FV reliable without undue cost or effort | Fair value through P&L | 11.14(c)(i) |
| Other 11.8(d) shares | Cost less impairment | 11.14(c)(ii) |
| Part II instruments | Fair value through P&L, except effective part of cash-flow-type hedges (OCI, 11.69) and unquoted equity (and linked contracts) not reliably measurable → cost less impairment | 11.54 |

- No deduction for costs to sell (11.14).
- **Amortised cost (11.15)** = initial amount − principal repayments ± cumulative EIR amortisation of initial-vs-maturity difference − impairment (assets). Current items with no stated rate that are not financing: (c) does not apply.
- **Effective interest rate (11.16)**: rate exactly discounting estimated future cash flows over expected life (or shorter period) to initial carrying amount; interest = opening carrying amount × EIR. Use Excel Goal Seek (module).
- Cash flow estimates include prepayment/call options and known incurred credit losses, not future credit losses (11.17). If a creditor can demand repayment at a date, the term cannot exceed that date (module notes). Expected prepayment shortens the amortisation period (Ex 67, Ex 75).
- Fees, points, transaction costs, premiums/discounts amortised over expected life, or to next repricing date where they relate to a repriced variable (11.18). Transaction costs on a variable-rate instrument are usually amortised over expected life (module); Ex 68 is a fee relating to an interest-free two-year period, amortised over two years.
- Variable rate: re-estimation alters the EIR; if initially at par, little effect on carrying amount (11.19, Ex 80). Module tip: amortise transaction costs on variable loans as a separate stream (Ex 81).
- **Revised estimates (11.20)**: recompute carrying amount as PV of revised cash flows at the **original** EIR; adjustment to P&L at revision date. For fixed-rate instruments the EIR is not updated; for variable-rate it is.
- Part II specifics: FV of a demand liability ≥ amount payable on demand discounted from first date payable (11.58, Ex 125–126). If reliable FV is lost for unquoted equity, last reliable FV becomes cost (11.56) — a change in circumstance, not policy; prior FV gains not reversed.
- **Impairment (11.21–11.26)** — applies only to assets at cost or amortised cost (not liabilities, not FVTPL):
  - Assess at each reporting date for **objective evidence** of a loss event after initial recognition (11.21). Loss events (11.22): significant financial difficulty; breach/default/delinquency; concession due to debtor's difficulty; probable bankruptcy/reorganisation; observable data on a measurable decrease in cash flows from a group (e.g. adverse national/local/industry conditions). Other evidence (11.23): adverse technological, market, economic or legal changes in the debtor's environment.
  - **Losses expected from future events, however likely, are not recognised** (module). General reserves, regulatory or tax provisions not supported by objective evidence are not impairment.
  - Individually assess all equity instruments and individually significant assets; others individually or grouped by similar credit risk characteristics (11.24). Historical loss rates must be adjusted for current observable conditions.
  - Measurement (11.25): amortised cost asset → carrying amount less PV of estimated cash flows at **original** EIR (current EIR for variable rate); short-term receivables not discounted if immaterial; collateralised assets reflect foreclosure cash flows less costs. Cost-less-impairment asset → carrying amount less best estimate (may be zero) of sale proceeds at reporting date (Section 12 techniques may help; difficulty is no excuse for omission).
  - After write-down, interest income is recognised at the rate used to measure the impairment (module).
  - Reduce directly or via an allowance account; loss in P&L either way.
  - Reversal (11.26): only if objectively related to a later event; capped at what amortised cost would have been without impairment; P&L.
- **Derecognition — assets (11.33–11.35)**: derecognise only when (a) rights expire or are settled; (b) substantially all risks and rewards transferred; or (c) some significant risks and rewards retained but **control** transferred (transferee has practical ability to sell the whole asset to an unrelated third party unilaterally and without restrictions) — then derecognise, recognise retained/created rights and obligations at fair value, allocate carrying amount by relative fair values, difference to P&L. If not derecognised: keep the asset, recognise a liability for consideration received, no offset (11.34). Non-cash collateral stays with the transferor unless it defaults; reclassify separately if the transferee may sell/repledge (11.35).
- **Derecognition — liabilities (11.36–11.38)**: only when extinguished (discharged, cancelled, expired). Exchange with substantially different terms, or substantial modification (whether or not due to debtor difficulty), = extinguishment + new liability; difference to P&L. Statute of limitations: derecognise only once the period has lapsed. In-substance defeasance or a third party assuming the debt without legal release does not extinguish.

## Presentation
- Statement of financial position line items (4.2): cash and cash equivalents (a); trade and other receivables (b); financial assets not shown elsewhere (c); trade and other payables (l); financial liabilities not shown elsewhere (m). Current/non-current split drives undiscounted vs amortised cost for non-financing items (11.14(a)).
- Allowance account presentation: receivables shown net of the allowance (module, Ex 86).
- No offset of a transferred-but-not-derecognised asset and its associated liability (11.34).
- Interest expense in finance costs; interest income and dividends typically in other income (module Ex 102, 109). FX gains/losses on foreign-currency payables in other income/expenses (Ex 102 policy).
- Hedge OCI amounts shown in OCI; net-investment hedge amounts are never reclassified (11.69).

3. **Loan with transaction costs (Ex 76)**: CU5,000 at 8%, expected repaid after 2 years, broker fee CU100 → initial CU4,900; EIR 9.139%; interest 447.82 then 452.18; carrying amount end of year 1 CU4,947.82.
4. **Change in estimated cash flows (Ex 82)**: CU5,000 4-year 8% loan, costs CU100, EIR 8.612%. At end of year 1 the SME decides to prepay half in each of the next two years. PV of revised flows (2,900 and 2,700) at 8.612% = CU4,958.85 vs amortised cost CU4,921.99 → Dr P&L CU36.86 / Cr loan.
5. **Impairment on restructuring (Ex 89)**: employee loan carrying CU476.19 at end of year 2; extended to 20X9 with only CU250 expected. PV at original 5% = CU205.68 (250 ÷ 1.05⁴); impairment CU270.51. Interest then accretes 10.28, 10.80, 11.34, 11.90 to CU250.
6. **Market-rate test for stepped coupons (Ex 59/79)**: CU1,250 loan with coupons 75/100/125/150/205; PV at 10% market = CU1,250.62 ≈ price, so measure at CU1,250; EIR 10.013%; year-1 interest CU125.16, carrying CU1,300.16.
7. **Substantial modification (Ex 100–101)**: 8.612% EIR loan, PV of original remaining flows CU4,921.99. (a) Rate cut 8%→5%, fee CU50: new PV incl. fee CU4,589.67, difference 6.8% → not extinguishment (account under 11.20). (b) All interest waived, principal deferred one year: PV incl. fee CU3,643.01, difference 26% → extinguishment: Dr old loan 4,921.99, Cr new loan 3,675.15 (5,000 ÷ 1.08⁴ at market rate), Cr cash 50, Cr gain 1,196.84.

## Review points and common errors
- Do not apply a historical percentage across all receivables or extend it to dissimilar assets (e.g. a 10% trade-debtor rate applied to an employee loan, Ex 83). Ageing matrices are acceptable only if they reflect actual delinquency for similar-risk customers, are reviewed regularly, and give a result not materially different from strict 11.24–11.25 (Ex 85).
- No "general provision" or expected-loss top-ups: impairment needs objective evidence of a past loss event; regulatory/tax reserves are not impairment.
- Granting extra time to a distressed debtor turns a short-term receivable into a financing: discount (Ex 87: CU1,000 ÷ 1.05 = CU952.38, impairment CU47.62).
- Restructurings that defer principal without full compensating interest cause impairment (Ex 88 cases (a)–(d)); only full interest-on-interest avoids it (case (e)) — and check it is realistic.
- 11.43(b) column must show the **cumulative** allowance (e.g. prior CU1,500 + current CU2,000 = CU3,500), and the age analysis must reconcile to the SFP.
- 11.43A uses contractual **undiscounted** cash flows including future interest — do not copy SFP carrying amounts; put demand items (overdrafts) in the earliest band.
- Transaction costs: include for amortised cost/cost items; expense for FVTPL Part I shares; exclude for Part II initial FV (Ex 122: CU5,000 not CU5,100).
- Below-market related-party or staff loans recognised at face value — must be discounted at a market rate (11.13B); allocate the difference (benefit, expense, intangible, or — Source silent on capital contribution treatment for parent loans - confirm).
- Overdrafts and other on-demand liabilities: not discounted (Ex 54, 64).
- Classification is fixed at inception (11.11A); when terms change, test 11.37 rather than reclassifying.
- Part II derivatives (FX forwards, swaps) are often omitted from the ledger because they cost nil — they must be recognised at inception and remeasured at FV through P&L.
- Check intra-group financial guarantees: nil-consideration intra-group → Section 21; fee-bearing or third-party → Part II.
- Do not separate embedded derivatives — the whole instrument is assessed (module; IFRS comparison).
- Module arithmetic slips to beware of when reusing examples: Ex 77 journal shows FCU72.3 but the footnote calculation is FCU72.73; Ex 90 computes the impairment at 31 December 20X4 but describes it as "for 20X5"; Ex 92 quotes a "would-have-been" amount of CU453.51 at 31 December 20X7 that does not follow from Ex 89's tables — recompute rather than copy.
- Hedge accounting is optional and needs contemporaneous documentation (risk, hedged item, instrument) — no documentation, no hedge accounting (11.62(a)).

### Hedge accounting in brief (11.61–11.71)
- Conditions (11.62): designated and documented; hedged risk in 11.63; instrument per 11.64; expected to be highly effective (assess at least at each annual reporting date; critical-terms match, ratio analysis, regression — module).
- Eligible risks (11.63): interest rate risk of an amortised-cost debt instrument; FX or interest rate risk in a firm commitment or highly probable forecast transaction; commodity price risk (held, or firm commitment / highly probable forecast); FX risk in a net investment in a foreign operation. **FX risk of an amortised-cost debt instrument is not eligible** — both sides already go to P&L under 30.10.
- Eligible instruments (11.64): interest rate swap, foreign currency swap, FX forward, commodity forward; external party; notional equals designated hedged amount; maturity not later than the hedged item/transaction; no prepayment, early termination or extension features.
- Fair value type (11.65–11.68): fixed-rate debt or commodity held — instrument FV change in P&L; hedged item adjusted for hedged-risk FV change through P&L; swap net settlements in P&L as accrued; on discontinuance, amortise the basis adjustment via EIR.
- Cash flow type (11.69–11.71): variable-rate debt, FX/commodity in firm commitment or forecast transaction, net investment — effective portion in OCI; excess cumulative change (ineffectiveness) in P&L; reclassify when hedged item affects P&L (never for net investment hedges); discontinue prospectively; reclassify OCI to P&L if forecast transaction no longer expected or hedged debt derecognised.
- Without hedge accounting (Ex 136) a receive-fixed swap on a fixed loan swings P&L: FV +2,500 in 20X4, −3,500 in 20X5 (illustrative figures).

## USD functional currency / KHR presentation currency
- **Step 1 — measure in USD (functional currency)**. A KHR-denominated bank balance, KHR receivable or KHR payable held by the USD entity is a foreign-currency **monetary item**: record at the spot rate on initial recognition (30.7; an average rate for a week or month is acceptable unless rates fluctuate significantly, 30.8), then remeasure in USD at the **closing rate** at each reporting date (30.9(a)); exchange differences on settlement or retranslation go to **profit or loss** (30.10; 11.10(b) confirms a foreign-currency payable remains a basic instrument with FX changes in P&L). This happens in the USD ledger before any translation to KHR.
- Amortised-cost foreign-currency loans: compute amortised cost and EIR in the currency of denomination, translate interest at the average/transaction rate and the closing balance at the closing rate, and post the balancing exchange difference to P&L (module Ex 77: FCU loan, exchange gain FCU13.91 in year 1 and loss FCU280.72 in year 2).
- Note: for a USD entity, KHR balances that are economically "local" are still foreign currency; USD balances are not retranslated in the ledger.
- Non-monetary financial items measured at fair value in a foreign currency (e.g. listed shares priced in KHR): translate at the rate on the date fair value was determined (30.9(c)); FX component follows the FV gain into P&L (30.11).
- Impairment of a KHR receivable: estimate recoverable cash flows in KHR, discount at original EIR where relevant, translate at closing rate (Source silent on ordering in module - confirm; follows 30.9(a)).
- Hedge accounting cannot be applied to the FX risk of a KHR loan or payable at amortised cost (11.63) — the natural P&L offset is the reason.
- Intra-group monetary items forming part of a net investment in a foreign operation: P&L in individual/separate statements, OCI in consolidated (30.12–30.13); trade balances never qualify.
- **Step 2 — translate USD statements into KHR (30.17–30.18)**: all financial assets and liabilities (including comparatives) at the closing USD/KHR rate for that SFP date; interest income/expense, FV gains, impairment losses, FX gains/losses at transaction-date rates or an average rate if rates do not fluctuate significantly (30.19). The resulting translation difference goes to OCI and accumulates in a separate equity component, **never reclassified** (30.18(c), 30.20). Do not confuse this KHR translation reserve with the 30.10 FX gains/losses already in the USD P&L.
- Translate disclosure tables (11.41 categories, 11.43 age analysis, 11.43A maturity analysis, 11.42 loan terms) at the closing rate so they reconcile to the KHR SFP; translate 11.48 income/expense items at the rates used for the statement of comprehensive income. Translate hedge OCI movements (11.75(c)–(d)) at the same rates as other OCI items (Source silent on detail - confirm).
- Disclose FX differences recognised in P&L (30.25(a), excluding those on FVTPL financial instruments), the translation reserve movement (30.25(b)), and that the presentation currency (KHR) differs from the functional currency (USD) with the reason (30.26).
- Hyperinflation: Section 31 only if the functional currency is hyperinflationary (30.21) — not expected for USD; Source silent on status - confirm.

## Differences from full IFRS Accounting Standards
- **Classification**: IFRS 9 uses business model + contractual cash flow characteristics; Section 11 classifies as basic or more complex by terms, with the 11.9ZA cash-flow principle but **no business-model test**.
- **Initial measurement**: IFRS 9 at fair value (except some trade receivables); Part I at transaction price unless trade receivable or financing; Part II at fair value.
- **Subsequent measurement**: IFRS 9 FVTPL, FVOCI or amortised cost, and no cost measurement; Section 11 amortised cost (Part I), FVTPL (Part II and reliably measurable shares), cost less impairment for unquoted shares. No FVOCI category.
- **Impairment**: IFRS 9 expected credit losses; Section 11 incurred losses on objective evidence.
- **Embedded derivatives**: IFRS 9 may separate; Section 11 never separates.
- **Hedge accounting**: IFRS 9 broader risks/instruments with effectiveness requirements; Section 11 only 11.63 risks and 11.64 instruments.
- **Disclosures**: much reduced vs IFRS 7 — e.g. no IFRS 7 credit/liquidity/market risk narrative or sensitivity analysis equivalent; 11.43A maps to IFRS 7 39(a)–(b); 11.43, 11.43B, 11.44 and 11.41(c)–(f) have no IFRS 7 equivalent; 11.45 maps to IFRS 7 42D(a), (b), (e).
