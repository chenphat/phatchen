# Section 28 — Employee Benefits (third edition)

> Sources: Educational Module 28 (third edition); IFRS for SMEs Accounting Standard (2025) paragraphs 28.1–28.43, transition A42; Section 30 paragraphs 30.7, 30.9–30.10, 30.18–30.20, 30.25–30.26 for the currency section.

## When this section matters in an SME compilation
- Payroll ledger: salaries, wages, employer social security, bonuses, staff benefits in kind (housing, cars, medical) — accruals at year-end (28.3–28.5).
- Leave balances (annual, sick) that carry forward or are paid out — accrual needed only if accumulating or vesting (28.6–28.7).
- Pension / provident / retirement-gratuity arrangements: classify defined contribution vs defined benefit (28.10–28.12).
- Long-service, sabbatical, multi-year bonus or deferred compensation schemes (28.29–28.30).
- Redundancy, restructuring, voluntary separation offers (28.31–28.37).

## What changed in the third edition
- Depth of the market in high quality corporate bonds is assessed **at a currency level** (28.17).
- Under the 28.19 simplification, the obligation is measured at the current termination amount, assuming all employees terminate at the reporting date (28.19(a)).
- Timing of termination benefit recognition aligned with restructuring costs in Section 21: earlier of (a) date the entity can no longer withdraw the offer and (b) date it recognises restructuring costs involving termination benefits (28.34–28.34B; 28.35 deleted).
- Disclosure requirements amended (e.g. 28.41(g) and (j) deleted; 28.41A–28.41E; 28.44 deleted).
- Transition: apply amended 28.19 retrospectively under Section 10, but no need to adjust carrying amounts of other assets (e.g. inventory, PPE) for employee benefit costs capitalised before the date of initial application (A42). Effective for periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027.

## Scope and key definitions
- **Employee benefits**: all forms of consideration given in exchange for service rendered by employees, including directors and management (28.1). Share-based payments go to Section 26 (28.2). Employees include full-time, part-time, permanent, casual or temporary staff; benefits may be paid to dependants or third parties (e.g. insurers).
- Four types (28.1): **short-term** (wholly due within 12 months after period-end of service, other than termination); **post-employment** (payable after completion of employment); **other long-term** (not wholly due within 12 months); **termination** (from entity's decision to terminate before normal retirement, or employee's acceptance of an offer).
- **Defined contribution plan** (28.10(a)): fixed contributions to a separate fund; no legal or constructive obligation to pay more if fund is insufficient. **Defined benefit plan** (28.10(b)): all other post-employment plans; actuarial and investment risk borne by entity. Indicators of DB: benefit formula not linked solely to contributions; guaranteed return; informal practices (e.g. history of inflation increases).
- Post-employment arrangements are within scope whether or not a separate entity exists, whether imposed by law or arising from entity action without a formal plan (28.9).
- **Multi-employer and state plans** (28.11): classify on terms incl. constructive obligations; if DB but insufficient information, account as DC and disclose (28.40).
- **Insured plans** (28.12): DC unless entity retains legal or constructive obligation to pay benefits directly or pay further amounts.
- **Plan assets**: assets held by a long-term employee benefit fund and qualifying insurance policies (IAS 19 definition may be used via 10.6).
- **Projected unit credit method**: each period of service gives rise to an additional unit of benefit entitlement, each unit measured separately to build up the final obligation.
- Payroll taxes levied on the employer (not consideration for service) are not employee benefits (Ex 4).

## Recognition
- **General principle** (28.3): recognise cost of benefits earned in the period as a liability (net of amounts paid; excess payment is an asset if it reduces future payments or will be refunded) and as an expense, unless another section requires capitalisation into inventory or PPE.
- **Accumulating compensated absences** (28.6): recognise as service increases entitlement; current liability. **Non-accumulating** (28.7): recognise when absence occurs.
- **Profit-sharing and bonuses** (28.8): only when a present legal or constructive obligation exists (no realistic alternative but to pay) and a reliable estimate can be made.
- **DC plans** (28.13): contribution payable for the period as liability (net of payments; prepayment = asset) and expense (or asset cost).
- **DB plans** (28.14): recognise net defined benefit liability; the net change in it during the period (excluding benefits paid and employer contributions) is the cost (28.23). Unvested benefits create an obligation; probability of vesting affects measurement, not existence (28.26).
- **Actuarial gains and losses** (28.24): recognised in the period they occur, either all in P&L or all in OCI — accounting policy election applied consistently to all DB plans and all actuarial gains and losses. If in OCI, present in the statement of comprehensive income.
- Plan introductions/changes: increase/decrease to P&L immediately; curtailments and settlements: gain or loss in P&L (28.21). Termination benefits may trigger a curtailment (28.33).
- **Plan surplus** (28.22): asset only to the extent recoverable via reduced contributions or refunds.
- **Reimbursements** (28.28): virtually certain reimbursement is a separate asset at fair value; expense may be presented net.
- **Other long-term** (28.30): net liability; net change in P&L entirely (no OCI option), unless capitalised.
- **Termination benefits** (28.32, 28.34): expense immediately; liability and expense at the earlier of (a) when the entity can no longer withdraw the offer and (b) when it recognises Section 21 restructuring costs involving termination benefits. Employee-accepted offers: earlier of acceptance and a restriction on withdrawal taking effect (28.34A). Entity decisions: when a plan meeting 28.34B criteria (unlikely to change; numbers, functions, locations, completion date identified; benefits specified in enough detail) is communicated to affected employees.
- Benefits under a plan are termination benefits if they result from the entity's decision to terminate and are not conditional on future service (28.31).

## Measurement
### Initial
- **Short-term**: undiscounted amount expected to be paid (28.5); no actuarial assumptions.
- Accumulating absences: undiscounted additional amount expected to be paid for unused entitlement at period-end (28.6) — reflect expected salary increase and expected forfeiture (Ex 10, 12).
- Non-accumulating absences: undiscounted salaries for the absence period (28.7).
- **DB liability** (28.15): PV of defined benefit obligation less fair value of plan assets (Section 12).
- DBO includes vested and unvested benefits and back-loaded formulas (28.16); actuarial assumptions unbiased, mutually compatible, best estimate (demographic: mortality, turnover, early retirement; financial: discount rate, salary, medical costs).
- **Discount rate** (28.17): market yields on high quality corporate bonds at reporting date; where no deep market in those bonds **for that currency**, government bond yields in that currency. Currency and term consistent with the benefit payments. Reflects time value only, not actuarial, investment or entity credit risk.
- **Method** (28.18): projected unit credit method if possible without undue cost or effort (reflects future salary increases).
- **Simplification** (28.19) if PUC involves undue cost or effort — for current employees: assume all terminate at the reporting date, ignore (i) future salary increases, (ii) future service, (iii) in-service mortality (but consider post-service life expectancy); and do not discount. Still include vested and unvested benefits. Requires 28.41(c) disclosures.
- No requirement for an independent actuary or an annual comprehensive valuation; between valuations, roll forward by adjusting for employee numbers and salary levels if principal assumptions have not changed significantly (28.20).
- Government-plan offsets reflected only if enacted before reporting date or predictable (28.27).
- **Termination benefits** (28.36): best estimate of expenditure to settle at reporting date; voluntary redundancy based on number expected to accept. Discount if due more than 12 months after period-end (28.37).
- **Group plans** (28.38): subsidiaries may recognise expense on a reasonable allocation of the group expense where the parent presents consolidated FS under IFRS for SMEs or full IFRS.

### Subsequent
- Components of DB cost (28.25): current service cost; interest on DBO; returns on plan assets and change in reimbursement rights; actuarial gains/losses; plan introductions/changes; curtailments/settlements.
- Practical: DB cost = closing net liability + benefits paid by entity + contributions − opening net liability (Ex 33–34). Interest cost and return on plan assets usually come from the actuarial report.
- Other long-term benefits remeasured each reporting date at PV less plan assets; all changes in P&L (28.30).

## Presentation
- Accumulating compensated absences presented as a **current liability** (28.6).
- DB net liability (or asset, limited by 28.22) in the statement of financial position; actuarial gains/losses either in P&L or OCI (28.24) — OCI amounts presented in the statement of comprehensive income.
- Other long-term employee benefit liabilities split current / non-current (module Ex 50).
- Reimbursement expense may be netted in P&L (28.28).
- Costs capitalised to inventory or PPE where required (28.3(b)).

## Worked examples from the module
1. **Accumulating sick leave (Ex 10)**: opening liability CU2,600; closing CU3,651 (Employee 1: 400 × 1.05 × 5 = 2,100; Employee 2: 310 × 1.02 × 4 = 1,265; Employee 4: 180 × 1.06 × 1.5 = 286). Charge **CU1,051**.
2. **LIFO sick leave (Ex 12)**: 100 staff, 5 days each; 8 expected to take 6.5 days → liability for **12 days** (1.5 × 8) only.
3. **PUC vs simplified (Ex 26/27)**: lump sum 1% of final salary per year; salary CU10,000 growing 7%; discount 10%. PUC closing obligations 89.53 / 196.96 / 324.99 / 476.65 / 655.40. Simplified: 100.00 / 214.00 / 343.47 / 490.00 / 655.40 (no discounting, current salary).
4. **DB cost (Ex 33/34/36)**: unfunded; closing DBO 1,000,000, opening 900,000, pensions paid 40,000 → cost **CU140,000**. Funded: plan assets 890,000 → 980,000, contributions 110,000 → return 20,000; expense = 20,000 + 110,000 − 10,000 = **CU120,000**. OCI policy with actuarial loss 50,000: OCI 50,000, P&L 90,000.
5. **Termination (Ex 46/47)**: legal minimum CU1m but constructive practice; best estimate **CU3m**. If payable 30 June 20X3, discounted at 16% over 18 months → **CU2,401,233**.
6. **Case study 1 total**: holiday leave 261,422 + long-service 587,892 + DC payable 100,000 + termination 1,350,000 = **CU2,299,314**.

## Review points and common errors
- Leave accrued for non-accumulating, non-vesting leave (no liability, Ex 13) — or not accrued where leave vests or carries forward.
- Leave carried forward beyond 12 months treated as short-term (should be other long-term, discounted, Ex 8/41).
- Accumulating leave measured without expected forfeiture or salary increase, or discounted (short-term must be undiscounted, 28.5).
- Bonuses accrued without a legal/constructive obligation, or not accrued despite established practice (28.8).
- Statutory or contractual retirement/seniority lump sums treated as DC or expensed on payment instead of measured as DB obligations (28.9–28.10).
- 28.19 simplification used without documenting undue cost or effort, or applied incorrectly (must still use life expectancy and include unvested benefits) (28.19, 28.41(c)).
- Mixed actuarial gains/losses policy across plans (28.24 requires consistency).
- Surplus recognised beyond recoverable amount (28.22).
- Termination liabilities recognised on board decision before communication / irrevocability, or omitted when a qualifying plan was communicated before year-end (28.34–28.34B).
- Employer payroll levies classified as employee benefits (Ex 4).
- Missing 33.6 key management personnel compensation disclosure (28.39 note).

## USD functional currency / KHR presentation currency
- **Ledger (USD)**: salaries and contributions incurred in KHR are recorded at the spot (or approximate average if rates stable) rate at the transaction date (30.7–30.8).
- Employee benefit liabilities settled in cash — accrued salaries, leave, bonuses, DC payables, DB obligations, long-service and **severance/seniority-type obligations payable in KHR** — are **monetary items**: retranslate to USD at the closing rate each reporting date, with exchange differences in P&L (30.9(a), 30.10). (The Standard and module do not address any specific country's severance or seniority regime — Source silent - confirm the local legal terms and whether they create post-employment, other long-term or termination benefits under 28.1.)
- Where a DB or other long-term obligation is payable in KHR, the discount rate must be consistent with the currency of the payments; assess depth of the high quality corporate bond market for KHR at the currency level and use government bond yields in KHR if there is no deep market (28.17). Do not use USD yields for KHR cash flows.
- Where a DB obligation is measured in KHR and remeasured in USD, the module's disclosure example shows "exchange differences on foreign plans" as a separate line in the DBO and plan-asset reconciliations (Ex 49) — present the KHR→USD retranslation effect under "all other changes" (28.41(e)(vi), (f)(iii)). Whether it is presented within the DB cost or separately as an exchange difference: Source silent - confirm.
- **Translation to KHR (30.18)**: liabilities and plan assets at the closing rate; staff costs, DB cost and OCI actuarial amounts at transaction-date or average rates (30.18(b), 30.19); resulting differences to the translation reserve in OCI, not recycled (30.18(c), 30.20). Keep the actuarial OCI and translation reserve as separate components.
- Disclose functional currency USD, presentation currency KHR and reason (30.26); exchange differences recognised in P&L (30.25(a)) include those on KHR employee benefit liabilities.

## Differences from full IFRS Accounting Standards
- IAS 19 always requires the projected unit credit method; Section 28 permits the 28.19 simplified approach when PUC involves undue cost or effort.
- Section 28 clarifies annual comprehensive valuations are usually unnecessary; roll-forward allowed (28.20).
- IAS 19 requires all remeasurements (actuarial gains/losses) in OCI; Section 28 permits P&L or OCI.
- IAS 19 requires asset-ceiling adjustments in OCI; Section 28 has a recognition limit (28.22) but does not specify OCI for its changes.
- IAS 19 defines actuarial gains and losses and their composition; Section 28 does not.
- Section 28 DB disclosures are less detailed.
- Short-term, DC, other long-term and termination benefits: principles shared; Section 28 has simpler language and less guidance.
