# Section 29 — Income Tax (third edition)

> Sources: Educational Module 29 (third edition); IFRS for SMEs Accounting Standard (2025) paragraphs 29.1–29.43, transition A43–A44. FX cross-references are to Section 30 and Educational Module 30.

## When this section matters in an SME compilation
- Every profit-making entity: current tax expense and payable or refund, prepayments and instalments of tax, and prior-year over- or under-provisions (29.4–29.6).
- Any difference between the carrying amounts in the books and the tax values. Common sources are accelerated tax depreciation, provisions deductible when paid, accrued interest taxed on receipt, revaluations and fair-value investment property, tax losses and credits (29.7–29.31).
- Items booked in OCI or equity, which need the tax to follow them: PPE revaluation surplus, share-issue costs, FX on a net investment (29.35).
- Dividends: withholding tax deducted from shareholders, and distributed/undistributed rate regimes (29.33–29.34).
- Disputed or unclear tax positions (29.34A–29.34D), and group members within the scope of Pillar Two (29.3A, 29.42–29.43).

## What changed in the third edition
- **Deferred tax assets for unrealised losses:** 29.16A (tax-law restrictions on sources of profit), 29.19(a) (exclude reversing deductions when testing future profit) and 29.19A (future profit may include recovering assets for more than their carrying amount if there is sufficient evidence that this is probable).
- **Uncertain tax treatments:** new 29.34A–29.34D.
- **Offsetting:** 29.37–29.37A are aligned with IAS 12, and the undue cost or effort exemption is removed.
- **Pillar Two:** a mandatory exception from deferred tax for Pillar Two income taxes (29.3A), with disclosures in 29.42–29.43. This was added by a 2023 amendment rather than the comprehensive review.
- Transition: apply 29.16A, 29.19(a) and 29.19A retrospectively, with relief to put the whole effect in opening retained earnings of the earliest comparative period, which must be disclosed (A43). Apply 29.34A–D retrospectively, or through cumulative catch-up in opening retained earnings at the date of initial application if hindsight would be needed (A44; Ex 112). The edition is effective for periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027.

## Scope and key definitions
- **Income tax (29.1):** all domestic and foreign taxes **based on taxable profit**, including withholding taxes payable by a subsidiary, associate or joint arrangement on distributions to the reporting entity. Excluded: sales taxes, VAT/GST, per-unit production taxes, payroll and social security taxes (Section 28), stamp duty, and taxes on emissions volume (Ex 1). A tax on a percentage of revenue may be an income tax if it is set as a surrogate for profit. That is a matter of judgement.
- **Current tax:** tax payable or recoverable on the taxable profit or loss of the current or past periods. **Deferred tax:** tax payable or recoverable in future periods from recovering or settling carrying amounts, and from carrying forward unused losses and credits (29.2).
- **Tax base of an asset (29.9):** the amount deductible against taxable benefits when the carrying amount is recovered. If those benefits are not taxable, the tax base equals the carrying amount.
- **Tax base of a liability (29.10):** the carrying amount less amounts deductible in future periods. For revenue received in advance, it is the carrying amount less the amount that will not be taxable in future.
- **Items with a tax base but no carrying amount (29.11):** for example, R&D expensed but deductible later.
- **Temporary difference (29.12):** carrying amount minus tax base. In consolidated statements use consolidated carrying amounts, and the tax base from the consolidated return or each entity's return as applicable.
- Government grants are accounted for under Section 24. Section 29 deals only with their tax effects (29.3).

## Recognition
### Current tax (29.4–29.5)
- Recognise a liability for unpaid tax on current and past periods. An excess paid is an asset (29.4).
- Recognise an asset for a tax loss that can be **carried back** (29.5). A loss carried forward is deferred tax (29.21). A loss that cannot be used at all gives no asset (Ex 11).
- A revision found before the statements are authorised for issue is an adjusting event (Section 32; Ex 6). A revision found after authorisation is a change in estimate, or a prior-period error only if it was a material error (Section 10; Ex 7). Preparers cannot choose freely between these treatments.

### Deferred tax: the temporary-difference approach step by step (Table 1)
| Step | Action | Paragraphs |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Determine the tax base of every asset and liability and of off-balance-sheet items such as R&D and share options | 29.9–29.11 |
| 2 | Temporary difference = carrying amount − tax base | 29.12–29.13 |
| 3 | Identify taxable temporary differences not covered by the exceptions | 29.14, 29.25 |
| 4 | Identify deductible temporary differences not covered by the exceptions. For investments, check whether they will reverse | 29.16, 29.26 |
| 5 | Test whether probable taxable profit will be available for the deductible differences | 29.16A–29.20 |
| 6 | Compute unused tax losses and credits | 29.21 |
| 7 | Test the probability of future profit for losses and credits | 29.21–29.22 |
| 8 | Measure at enacted or substantively enacted rates, consistent with the expected manner of recovery, without discounting | 29.27–29.32 |
| 9 | Recognise. Deferred tax expense = closing net DTL/DTA − opening, allocated to P&L, OCI or equity | 29.35 |
| 10–12 | Reassess unrecognised DTAs, reassess recognised DTAs, and remeasure for rate changes. These feed 29.39(d), (e) and (g) | 29.23, 29.31 |

Sign logic (Table 2): an **asset** with carrying amount above its tax base, or a **liability** with carrying amount below its tax base, gives a taxable temporary difference and a **DTL**. The reverse gives a deductible temporary difference and a **DTA**.

### Recognition exceptions
- **DTL (29.14):** none on (a) the initial recognition of goodwill, or (b) the initial recognition of an asset or liability in a transaction that is not a business combination and affects neither accounting nor taxable profit. Later amortisation or impairment of that original difference also stays unrecognised (Ex 51, Ex 53). If goodwill or a licence is tax-deductible on an accelerated basis, the later difference **is** recognised (Ex 52: DTL CU4; Ex 54: DTL CU100).
- **DTA (29.16):** recognised only to the extent that it is probable taxable profit will be available, and not for the same initial-recognition case. Consider whether tax law restricts the sources of profit (29.16A).
- **Sources of probable profit:** in order, (1) taxable temporary differences for the same authority and the same entity that reverse in the same period or within the loss carryback or carryforward window (29.18); (2) future taxable profit, excluding reversing deductions and new deductible differences (29.19(a)); (3) tax planning opportunities (29.19(b)). A history of recent losses requires sufficient taxable temporary differences or "convincing other evidence" (29.20–29.22).
- **Investments in subsidiaries, branches, associates and joint arrangements:** no DTL if the investor controls the timing of reversal **and** reversal is probably not in the foreseeable future (29.25). A DTA is recognised only if reversal is in the foreseeable future and profit is available (29.26). Outside differences arise from undistributed profits, **exchange-rate changes** and impairment (29.24).
- **Pillar Two (29.3A):** never recognise or disclose deferred tax relating to Pillar Two income taxes. Current Pillar Two tax is recognised and disclosed (29.43).

## Measurement
### Initial
- **Current tax:** the amount expected to be paid or recovered using rates and laws **enacted or substantively enacted** by the reporting date. A rate is substantively enacted when the remaining steps have not affected the outcome in the past and are unlikely to (29.6). In the US, substantive enactment generally means legal enactment because of the presidential veto.
- **Deferred tax:** enacted or substantively enacted rates expected to apply when the difference reverses (29.27). With graduated rates, use the **average rate** expected for the reversal periods (29.28; Ex 77–Ex 79).
- **Manner of recovery (29.29):** the rate and tax base must be consistent with use versus sale, for example a capital gains rate. Non-depreciable revalued assets are assumed to be recovered through **sale**. Fair-value investment property is presumed to be recovered through sale, rebuttable only if it is depreciable and held to consume its benefits (29.30).
- **No discounting** of current or deferred tax (29.32).
- **Distributed versus undistributed rates:** use the undistributed rate until a dividend liability is recognised (29.33; Ex 95–Ex 96). **Withholding tax** on dividends paid is charged to equity as part of the dividend (29.34; Ex 97).

### Subsequent
- Review DTAs at each reporting date, write them down if recovery is no longer probable, and reverse the write-down if it becomes probable again (29.31). Recognise a previously unrecognised DTA when recovery becomes probable (29.23).
- Remeasure existing balances for rate changes substantively enacted by the reporting date. A rate announced after year-end is a non-adjusting event (32.11(h); Ex 13; Test question 9).

### Uncertain tax treatments (29.34A–29.34D)
1. Group uncertainties or treat them separately, depending on which better predicts the resolution (29.34B; Ex 98).
2. Assume the authority **will examine** with full knowledge, whatever the likelihood of an audit (29.34C). Once the authority's right to examine expires, the treatment is no longer uncertain.
3. If it is probable (more likely than not) that the authority accepts the treatment, use the treatment as filed. If not probable, reflect the uncertainty using the **most likely amount**, which suits binary outcomes (Ex 99), or the **expected value**, which suits a range (Ex 101: probability-weighted extra tax CU57).
4. Keep judgements consistent across current tax and tax bases (Ex 100: the tax base of a CU800 accrual is CU800, so no DTA).
5. New information is a change in estimate, applied prospectively (29.34D). In Ex 102 a new rule gives a most likely amount of CU60, and the CU3 difference goes to the next year's expense.

## Presentation
- **29.35:** tax follows the item that gave rise to it. Tax on items in continuing operations, discontinued operations, OCI or equity is recognised in the same place. Only total tax needs allocating, and splitting current from deferred tax can be arbitrary. OCI items include FX on foreign operations, actuarial gains, hedge fair-value changes and revaluation surplus. Equity items include retrospective policy changes and error corrections, the equity component of compound instruments, and share-issue costs (Ex 104: CU200 tax credit to equity).
- **29.36:** deferred tax assets and liabilities are never classified as current.
- **29.37:** offset current tax assets and liabilities only with a legally enforceable right **and** an intention to settle net or simultaneously.
- **29.37A:** offset deferred tax assets and liabilities only with an enforceable right to offset current tax **and** the same authority, and either the same taxable entity or different entities that intend to settle net. Test question 12: Jurisdiction A nets to a DTL of CU2,000 and Jurisdiction B to a DTA of CU4,000, presented separately. Capital-loss DTAs offset only against capital-gain DTLs. Offset in the SFP does not override the allocation between P&L and OCI.

## Worked examples from the module
- **Ex 3 — accelerated depreciation:** machine CU600, written off fully for tax in year 1, accounting depreciation CU200 a year, 30%. Current tax 180/360/360. DTL 120 → 60 → nil. Deferred tax charge (credit) 120/(60)/(60). The total charge is 300 each year, which is 30% of CU1,000.
- **Ex 4 — current tax computation:** profit 150,000 − exempt royalty 20,000 + non-deductible entertainment 5,000 + bad-debt provision increase 500 − excess tax depreciation 8,000 = taxable profit 127,500 × 15% = **CU19,125**.
- **Ex 79 — graduated rates:** average rates of 17.96%, 18.11%, 19.12% and 19.12% by reversal year. DTL on CU5,000 interest = **CU898**. DTA on CU60,000 of R&D (20k/20k/10k/10k) = **CU11,038**.
- **Ex 63 — DTA supported by profits:** warranty CU50,000 against taxable temporary differences of CU38,000. With profits of about CU1m a year, recognise the full DTA.
- **Ex 103 — tax on an OCI item:** CU10,000 FX loss on a net investment in OCI, taxable profit CU500,000, rate 20%. Current tax of CU100,000 is split into P&L CU102,000 and OCI (CU2,000).

## Review points and common errors
- Tax on a current-year item is computed at a rate announced after year-end (Ex 13, Test question 9), or the deferred-tax rate is not updated for a change already substantively enacted (Ex 14).
- Deferred tax is classified as current, or DTAs and DTLs are netted across authorities or entities (29.36–29.37A).
- Current or deferred tax is discounted (29.32).
- DTL is recognised on goodwill or on the initial recognition of a non-deductible asset, or the DTL on accelerated **deductible** goodwill or licence is missed (Ex 51–Ex 54).
- DTA on losses is recognised despite a recent-loss history without convincing evidence (29.22; Ex 68).
- Future profit used in the DTA test includes the reversing deductions themselves (29.19(a)).
- Tax on OCI or equity items such as revaluations, net-investment FX and share-issue costs is left in P&L (29.35).
- Withholding tax on dividends **paid** is treated as tax expense instead of part of the dividend (29.34).
- Pillar Two deferred tax is recognised, or the 29.42 statement is omitted.
- The rate reconciliation (29.40(c)) or the unrecognised-DTA disclosure (29.40(f)) is missing.
- A post-authorisation correction is treated as a prior-period error when it is a change in estimate (Ex 7).

## USD functional currency / KHR presentation currency
**Current tax in the USD ledger:**
- Current tax assets and liabilities are **monetary items** (Module 30 note to 30.9).
- If the liability is assessed or payable in KHR, record the expense at the spot or average rate (30.7–30.8). Retranslate the unpaid KHR balance at the closing rate, with the difference in **P&L** as an exchange difference rather than as tax expense (30.9(a), 30.10). Include it in the 30.25(a) total.
- Instalments paid in KHR are recorded at the rate on the payment date.

**Deferred tax when the tax base is measured in KHR but the ledger is in USD:**
- Section 29 builds the temporary difference by comparing the carrying amount with the tax base (29.9–29.12). It works through a foreign-currency loan only where FX gains are taxable on repayment (Ex 35: carrying amount CU7,200, tax base CU8,000, taxable TD of CU800).
- Neither Module 29 nor Module 30 says how to treat **non-monetary assets whose tax base is fixed in KHR** while the carrying amount is in USD at historical rates. Specifically, they do not say whether the KHR tax base is retranslated at the closing rate each year, creating deferred tax on exchange movements, or where that movement is recognised. **Source silent - confirm.**
- Whether a deferred tax balance denominated in KHR is itself retranslated as a monetary item is also not addressed in the sources. **Source silent - confirm.**

**Translation of the tax lines into KHR presentation (30.18–30.19):**
| Line | Rate |
|---|---|
| Current tax payable or receivable, DTA, DTL | Closing KHR/USD rate at each SFP date, with comparatives at their own closing rate |
| Income tax expense (current and deferred) | Transaction or average rate. Ex 28 (Module 30) translated the CU4,495 tax expense at the 2.2 average = LCA9,889 |
| Tax on OCI items | Same rate basis as the OCI item. Allocation per 29.35 |
| Income tax paid (cash flow statement) | Rate at the payment date (Module 30 Case study 3: tax paid 126 × 2.05 = 258.3) |

- The 30.18(c) translation difference arises only from expressing USD amounts in KHR. Translation "does not change the measurement of the underlying items" (Module 30 note to 30.18). The sources do not identify any current or deferred tax arising on that presentation difference. **Source silent - confirm** with local tax law before recognising tax in OCI on the translation reserve.
- The rate reconciliation (29.40(c)) and the deferred tax movement table (29.40(e)) are prepared in USD and then translated. The translation effect on opening DTA/DTL balances shows up in the 30.18(c) difference, so any reconciling "exchange difference" line in the 29.40(e) movement table should be labelled as such. How to present that line is **not specified in the sources - confirm**.

## Differences from full IFRS Accounting Standards
- Section 29 uses the same principles as IAS 12 in simpler language. It adds that "substantively enacted" means the remaining steps have not affected the outcome in the past and are unlikely to.
- Section 29's initial-recognition exemption does **not** exclude transactions that give rise to equal taxable and deductible temporary differences. IAS 12 does exclude them.
- Section 29 prohibits discounting of **current and deferred** tax. IAS 12 prohibits discounting of deferred tax only.
- Section 29 has guidance on withholding tax on dividends paid, which IAS 12 does not. IAS 12 requires the tax consequences of dividends to be recognised where the transactions that generated the distributable profits were recognised, and Section 29 has no such requirement.
- Section 29 has simplified presentation. Disclosure mapping: IAS 12.79–81 ≈ 29.39–29.40, 82A = 29.40(g), 88A–88B = 29.42–29.43. IAS 12 paragraphs 81(f), (h)–(k), 82, 87–87C, 88, 88C and 88D have no equivalent.
