# Section 2 — Concepts and Pervasive Principles (third edition)

> Sources: Educational Module 2 (third edition); IFRS for SMEs Accounting Standard (2025) paragraphs 2.1–2.130, with cross-references to 3.15–3.16A, 10.3–10.6, 8.6–8.7 and Table 1 of the Standard's Introduction.

## When this section matters in an SME compilation
- No section of the Standard deals with a transaction, or the relevant section is silent. Under 10.4–10.5 you then use the definitions, recognition criteria and measurement concepts in Section 2. Under 10.6 you may also look to full IFRS.
- Materiality calls: whether to correct an error, aggregate line items, leave out a disclosure, or not apply a policy to an immaterial item (10.3).
- A preparer or client wants an amount the definitions do not support: "general reserves", self-insurance provisions, profit smoothing, expected future losses.
- Substance over legal form: sale and repurchase, related-party purchases off market terms, treasury shares, NCI transactions.
- Any use of an undue cost or effort exemption (2.28–2.31). It must be disclosed.

## What changed in the third edition
- Section 2 was **rewritten to align with the 2018 Conceptual Framework**, and its language was simplified (Module 2, "What has changed"; Table 1, item 4).
- **Asset definition** is now "a present economic resource controlled by an entity as a result of past events". An economic resource is "a right that has the potential to produce economic benefits" (2.44–2.45). The module says this definition "has been updated from that in the second edition".
- **Liability definition** is now "present obligation to transfer an economic resource as a result of past events" (2.49). Neither definition contains a probability threshold. Where thresholds exist, they sit in the individual sections.
- **Recognition criteria changed.** The 2nd edition test (probable flow of benefits plus reliable measurement) is gone. Recognition now asks whether recognising the item gives **relevant** information that is a **faithful representation**, taking account of existence uncertainty, low probability of flows and measurement uncertainty (2.68–2.75; module notes to 2.68–2.69).
- New concepts: unit of account (2.57–2.58), executory contracts (2.59–2.60), derecognition (2.76–2.79), measurement bases and the factors for choosing between them (2.80–2.114), and presentation and disclosure as communication tools, including classification, OCI and aggregation (2.117–2.130).
- **Prudence** is reintroduced as support for neutrality (2.17).
- **Materiality** uses the "omitting, misstating or **obscuring**" definition (2.13). The same definition appears in 3.16, which adds "individually or in combination with other information… in the context of its financial statements taken as a whole". 3.15A (new) bans obscuring material information with immaterial information.
- **The accrual basis moved out of Section 2 into 3.16A** (Table 1, item 7). Cite 3.16A, not Section 2, for the accrual basis.
- **The old paragraph numbers no longer apply.** In the 2nd edition, materiality and the accrual basis had their own paragraphs in Section 2. In the 3rd edition, **2.6 is about economic resources and claims, not materiality**. Cite 2.13 / 3.16 for materiality.
- Transition: apply retrospectively under Section 10. Effective for periods beginning on or after 1 January 2027, and early application is permitted (Module 2, Transition).

## Scope and key definitions
- **Precedence (2.2):** where Section 2 conflicts with another section, the other section wins. Example (module Ex 1): goodwill cannot be given an indefinite life on "faithful representation" grounds, because Section 18 deems all intangibles finite-lived.
- **Objective (2.3):** give existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors useful information for decisions about providing resources. It covers resources and claims and changes in them, and management's stewardship (2.4, 2.6–2.7). Financial statements cannot give users everything they need (2.5).
- **Reporting entity (2.39–2.41):** need not be a legal entity. A parent plus subsidiaries gives consolidated statements. Two or more entities with no parent–subsidiary link give combined statements. 2.41(c) requires a description of how the boundary was set.
- **Going concern (2.38):** the normal assumption. If another basis is used, describe it (3.8–3.9). The module notes that 3.8 treats "foreseeable future" as at least, but not limited to, 12 months from the reporting date.
- **Reporting period and comparatives (2.33–2.34):** at least one preceding period, unless the Standard says otherwise. Forward-looking information is disclosed only when it relates to existing assets, liabilities or equity, or to period income and expenses, and is useful (2.35). Management strategy is not usually disclosed (2.36).

### Qualitative characteristics (2.8–2.24)
| Type | Characteristic | Compiler test |
|---|---|---|
| Fundamental | **Relevance** (2.11–2.12): predictive and/or confirmatory value | Boilerplate may not be relevant. An example is a policy note for impairment of intangibles when there are none (module notes). |
| Fundamental | **Materiality** (2.13): entity-specific aspect of relevance; nature or magnitude | No uniform threshold. Both quantitative and qualitative. |
| Fundamental | **Faithful representation** (2.14–2.18): complete, neutral, free from error; substance over form | Prudence: do not overstate assets or income, and do not understate them either (2.17). "Reliability does not equal precision." |
| Enhancing | Comparability (2.21) | Consistency helps achieve it. Disclose policies and changes in them. |
| Enhancing | Verifiability (2.22) | A range with probabilities can be verified. |
| Enhancing | Timeliness (2.23) | Older information is less useful. |
| Enhancing | Understandability (2.24) | **Never** a reason to leave out complex material information (Ex 7: deferred tax cannot be skipped). |
| Constraint | Cost (2.25–2.27) | Benefits must justify costs. |

**Order of application (2.19):** (a) identify the economic phenomenon, (b) identify the most relevant information about it, (c) check that the information is available and faithful. If it is not, move to the next most relevant type. The enhancing characteristics act as tie-breakers (2.20).

### Materiality (2.13; 3.15–3.16; 10.3)
- **Definition:** "Information is material if omitting, misstating or obscuring it could reasonably be expected to influence decisions that the primary users… make on the basis of those financial statements". It is entity-specific and depends on nature or magnitude or both. The IASB cannot set a uniform quantitative threshold.
- Users are assumed to have reasonable knowledge and to study the information with reasonable diligence (module notes).
- An immaterial item need not have the accounting policy applied to it (10.3), and an immaterial disclosure need not be given (module notes to 2.13).
- Aggregation: items that are not individually material are aggregated. An item that does not merit a separate line may still merit a separate note (module notes; 2.129–2.130; 3.15). Do not obscure material items (3.15A).
- Practice Statement 2 *Making Materiality Judgements* (2017) is non-mandatory and not written for SMEs, but the module says it "might still provide useful guidance".

### Elements (2.42–2.65)
| Element | Definition (paragraph) | Key tests |
|---|---|---|
| Asset | Present economic resource controlled by the entity as a result of past events (2.44) | It must be a **right** (2.46–2.47). Control means the present ability to direct use and obtain benefits, including the ability to prevent others (2.48). Benefits need not be certain or likely (2.45). Own shares are not assets. |
| Liability | Present obligation to transfer an economic resource as a result of past events (2.49) | Three conditions (2.50): an obligation exists (no practical ability to avoid, legal or constructive, 2.51); it is to transfer a resource (2.52–2.53); it is present and from a past event (2.55–2.56). |
| Equity | Residual interest in assets after deducting all liabilities (2.61) | Not measured directly (2.115). Can be negative (module notes). |
| Income | Increases in assets or decreases in liabilities that increase equity, other than contributions from equity holders (2.62) | Covers both revenue and gains. |
| Expenses | Decreases in assets or increases in liabilities that decrease equity, other than distributions to equity holders (2.63) | Covers both expenses and losses. |

- **Unit of account (2.57–2.58):** you may use one unit for recognition and another for measurement. An example is assessing receivables impairment by customer, not by invoice (module notes).
- **Executory contracts (2.59–2.60):** a combined right and obligation. It is an asset if the terms are favourable and a liability if unfavourable. Recognition depends on the measurement basis and any onerous test.

## Recognition
- Only items that meet an element definition are recognised. Not every such item is recognised (2.68).
- **Disclosure does not cure non-recognition** (2.69).
- Recognise when doing so gives **relevant** information (2.70–2.71) that is a **faithful representation** (2.72–2.73).
  - Existence uncertainty, a low probability of flows, or an exceptionally wide range of outcomes may make recognition irrelevant.
  - Very high measurement uncertainty may prevent a faithful representation.
  - Explanatory notes may be needed either way (2.71, 2.75).
- An item can qualify later (2.74).
- Contingent assets have a higher hurdle (virtually certain) under Section 21 (module notes to 2.71).
- **Matching is not an objective.** Nothing goes on the SFP unless it meets an element definition (module notes to 2.67).
- **Derecognition (2.76–2.79):** an asset is derecognised when control is lost and a liability when the present obligation ends. Continue recognising any retained component. An apparent transfer in which the entity keeps significant exposure to variations in benefits may mean the entity still controls the asset (module notes).
- **Accrual basis (3.16A, relocated from Section 2):** prepare the financial statements, except cash flow information, on the accrual basis. Items are recognised when they meet the definitions and recognition criteria.

## Measurement
### Initial
- **Choose a measurement basis first** (2.80–2.82). The right basis is specific to the item. The module notes that cost at initial recognition is normally equal to fair value, but the formal basis still matters because it drives subsequent measurement.
- **Historical cost (2.83–2.84):**
  - Asset: consideration paid plus transaction costs.
  - Liability: consideration received minus transaction costs.
- **Current value bases (2.88–2.91):**
  - **Fair value** (2.89): exit price; not increased by transaction costs. Measure it under Section 12.
  - **Current cost** (2.90): consideration plus transaction costs at the measurement date.
  - **Value in use** (assets) and **fulfilment value** (liabilities) (2.91): entity-specific present values that exclude transaction costs.
- **Not on market terms (module Ex 34):** a shareholder sells a machine worth CU1,000,000 for CU700,000. Recognise the asset at CU1,000,000 and a CU300,000 shareholder contribution in equity.

### Subsequent
- Historical cost of an asset is updated for depreciation or amortisation, receipts, impairment and interest accrual (2.85).
- Historical cost of a liability is updated for fulfilment, onerous increases and interest accrual (2.86).
- Amortised cost is a form of historical cost for financial instruments. Variable-rate instruments update the discount rate (2.87).
- Other methods used in the Standard: selling price less costs to complete and sell for impaired inventory, and recoverable amount for impaired non-current assets (module notes to 2.87).

**Factors in choosing a basis (2.99–2.114):**
- Relevance (2.102–2.105):
  - For items sensitive to market factors, historical cost may not be relevant.
  - For items that produce cash flows directly, current value is likely more relevant.
  - For assets used in combination (PPE, inventory), historical or current cost is likely to be relevant.
  - For collect-contractual-cash-flow activity, amortised cost is relevant.
- Faithful representation (2.106–2.109): use the same basis for linked items. Measurement uncertainty is different from outcome uncertainty and existence uncertainty.
- Enhancing characteristics and cost (2.110–2.114): consistency aids comparability. More bases mean less understandability. A change of basis needs explanation.

### Undue cost or effort (2.28–2.31)
- It applies **only where a specific requirement offers it** (2.28). It cannot be used by analogy (module notes).
- The test is met when the incremental cost (for example valuers' fees) or effort (for example staff endeavours) **substantially exceeds the benefit to users** (2.29). SMEs use a **lower threshold** than publicly accountable entities.
- It is judged at initial recognition. If the exemption also covers subsequent measurement, it is **re-judged at each later date** (2.30). The module's example is 11.14(c)(i).
- It is a lower hurdle than "impracticable", which requires every reasonable effort (module notes).
- If the information is already held or cheap to get, the exemption is **not** available. Example (Ex 8): the tax authority already requires an annual certified fair value of investment property, so the Section 16 exemption is unavailable.
- **Disclose** the fact that the exemption was applied and the reasons (2.31). The 19.16 exemption is the exception and is disclosed under 19.38.

## Presentation
- **Classification (2.120–2.122):** classify by nature, role or function, or measurement basis. Do not combine dissimilar items. Separate components where that gives more useful information.
- **P&L and OCI (2.123–2.125):** P&L is the primary performance statement. Use OCI **only where the Standard explicitly permits or requires it**. Reclassification from OCI happens only where an individual section permits it.
- **Offsetting (2.126):** offsetting groups an asset and a liability that are separate units of account into one net amount on the SFP. It is "generally not appropriate unless required or permitted by a specific section".
  - Defined benefit plans: the net liability is required by 28.15, which overrides 2.126 (Ex 36).
  - Income tax: 29.37–29.37A.
  - Reimbursements against provisions: Section 21.
- **Equity classification (2.127–2.128):** separate classes or components with different characteristics or legal restrictions, for example distributable reserves.
- **Aggregation (2.129–2.130):** balance too much detail against too little. Primary statements are summarised and notes are less aggregated.
- **Communication principles (module notes):** entity-specific information beats boilerplate. Duplication reduces understandability.

## Worked examples from the module
1. **Materiality (Ex 2–4).**
   - A depreciation error of CU150 against profit before tax of CU600,000 is not material, so no 10.21 restatement.
   - If correcting it would breach a covenant on a significant long-term loan, it is material (Ex 3).
   - A systemic DBO error with a cumulative overstatement of CU600,000 in opening retained earnings, against total equity of CU950,000, is material (Ex 4).
2. **Substance over form (Ex 5–6).** The yacht's fair value is CU2,000,000. It is sold for CU1,000,000 with repurchase at CU1,080,000 (incremental borrowing rate about 8%). Recognise a CU1,000,000 secured liability and CU80,000 finance cost under the EIR method. The answer is the same where the SME only has an *option* to repurchase, because a strike price far below fair value means the option will almost certainly be exercised.
3. **Not liabilities (Ex 21–22).**
   - Self-insurance of about CU100,000 every ten years: no CU10,000 annual provision, because there is no past event.
   - A ski resort's "good-year" provision: not allowed, even where tax law permits deferral.
4. **Revenue or gain (Ex 28–31).**
   - Inventory sold for CU1,500 with carrying amount CU1,000: revenue CU1,500 and cost of sales CU1,000 (gross).
   - Machine sold for CU1,500 with carrying amount CU1,000: gain CU500 (net).
   - Machine sold for CU900: loss CU100.
5. **Offsetting overridden (Ex 36).** DBO CU200,000 and plan assets CU180,000 give a net liability of CU20,000 under 28.15. Section 2 alone would present them gross.

## Review points and common errors
- Citing the 2nd edition's paragraph numbers for materiality or the accrual basis. The correct citations are now **2.13 / 3.16** and **3.16A**.
- Recognising "general reserves", equalisation or self-insurance provisions, or provisions for expected future losses. These are prohibited because there is no present obligation (module notes to 2.55–2.56; Ex 21–22). Check for onerous contracts instead (21A.2), and treat expected losses as a possible impairment indicator (Section 27).
- Using "prudence" to understate profit (Q10). Neutrality forbids both overstatement and understatement (2.17).
- Leaving out deferred tax, fair value or other complex items "because users won't understand" (Ex 7, Ex 8).
- Claiming undue cost or effort where the Standard offers no exemption, where the information already exists, or without the 2.31 disclosure. Exemptions on recurring measurements must be re-assessed every year (2.30).
- Netting balances (receivables against payables, bank overdraft against cash) without a section that permits it (2.126).
- Putting items in OCI that no section permits (2.124).
- Boilerplate policies for items the entity does not have (module notes to 2.12).
- Showing own shares as an investment asset (Ex 26).
- Booking off-market related-party purchases at the invoice price (Ex 34).
- Relying on disclosure to cure a failure to recognise (2.69).

## USD functional currency / KHR presentation currency
- Apply all Section 2 concepts in the **functional currency (USD)**. Recognition, element definitions, measurement bases and undue cost or effort are all judged on the USD ledger. Translation into KHR is a presentation step under 30.17. It is not a change of measurement basis.
- **Translation (30.18):**
  - All assets and liabilities, including comparatives, at the **closing rate** of each SFP date.
  - Income and expenses at transaction-date rates, or an average rate if rates do not fluctuate significantly (30.19).
  - Resulting differences go to **OCI** and accumulate in a separate component of equity, **never reclassified to P&L**.
- This is one of the cases where the Standard "explicitly permits or requires" OCI (2.124). The translation reserve is a separately classified equity component (2.127–2.128).
- The translation reserve arises from (a) rate differences between P&L items and closing rates, and (b) retranslating opening net assets at a new closing rate (30.20).
- **A movement in a historical-cost asset's KHR amount is translation, not remeasurement.** For example, PPE at historical cost in USD shows a different KHR amount each year-end only because the closing rate changed.
- Materiality is judged in the context of the financial statements as a whole (3.16). The source does not say whether to set a threshold in the functional or the presentation currency. Source silent - confirm.
- Disclose: the presentation currency; the fact that it differs from the functional currency (USD); and the reason (30.26). Also disclose the exchange differences in P&L and the amount classified in the separate component of equity (30.25).
- If USD were ever hyperinflationary, 30.21 / Section 31 would apply. Not expected for USD. Source silent on any KHR hyperinflation assessment.

## Differences from full IFRS Accounting Standards
- Section 2 is based on the 2018 Conceptual Framework, which full IFRS has applied since periods beginning on or after 1 January 2020.
- The main differences (module, Comparison section):
  - (a) **2.2 precedence.** Individual sections override Section 2. The Conceptual Framework instead stands separately from IFRS Standards.
  - (b) The **undue cost or effort exemption** (2.28–2.31), which has no counterpart in the Conceptual Framework.
- Other differences are mainly in the level of detail.
