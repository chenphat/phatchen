# Procedure: identifying SRROs and material information (IFRS S1)

Anchors: S1.17–19, S1.30, S1.54–59, B1–B37; ISSB educational material *Sustainability-related risks and
opportunities and the disclosure of material information* (Nov 2024); PwC SRG chapter 4 (2026).
Working papers: S1-03 (SRRO register), S1-04 (material information).

## Key concepts (get these right first)

- **Two steps, not one.** S1 first identifies SRROs that could reasonably be expected to affect
  prospects, then applies *materiality to information* about them. S1 does not call risks "material".
- **Prospects** = cash flows, access to finance, cost of capital, over short/medium/long term (S1.3).
- **Primary users** = existing and potential investors, lenders and other creditors, as a group (S1.18).
- **Sources of SRROs**: dependencies on and impacts on resources and relationships (natural,
  manufactured, intellectual, human, social, financial) across the value chain (S1.B2–B5). Example
  (B3): a business dependent on clean water faces risk if the resource degrades — even if others cause it.
- **Impact materiality is not required** under S1. Record impacts (GRI 3/ESRS) in a separate column if
  the entity also reports under GRI; never let the impact score drive the ISSB conclusion.
- **Reasonable and supportable information without undue cost or effort** (S1.B6–B10): internal
  (risk register, budgets, ERM, complaints) and external (SASB, peers, regulators, investor queries,
  scientific data). No exhaustive search; more useful information warrants more effort (BC17).

## Step-by-step procedure

### Step 1 — Understand the entity (S1-03 tab "Context")
Business model, products, sites, revenue by segment, key suppliers/customers, financing structure,
strategy documents, ERM register, investor/lender questions, regulator letters. Map the value chain
(upstream → own operations → downstream) and resource dependencies.

### Step 2 — Long-list potential SRROs
Sources in order:
1. SASB Standard(s) for the industry — "shall refer to and consider" topics and metrics (S1.55(a)).
   Document why any SASB topic is excluded.
2. IFRS S2 / Industry-based Guidance for climate.
3. Optional: CDSB guidance (water, biodiversity), TNFD LEAP for nature, recent investor-focused
   pronouncements, peers in the same industry and geography (S1.55(b), 58).
4. GRI/ESRS: may inform *what information* to give, not *which SRROs* exist (S1.BC136–137).
5. Stakeholder engagement is not required by S1 but helps test completeness.

### Step 3 — Assess each SRRO (likelihood × magnitude, by horizon)
Score on 1–5 scales. Consider completed mitigation; ignore planned future actions (SRG 4.6 practice).
A risk may be "reasonably expected" from the users' perspective even if management disagrees.

| Score | Likelihood (within horizon) | Magnitude (effect on prospects) — calibrate to entity |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Remote (<5%) | <0.5% of revenue or equity; no financing effect |
| 2 | Unlikely (5–25%) | 0.5–1% revenue/EBITDA; minor covenant headroom effect |
| 3 | Possible (25–50%) | 1–3% revenue/EBITDA; could change cost of funds |
| 4 | Likely (50–80%) | 3–5%; could affect access to a market, licence or financing |
| 5 | Almost certain (>80%) | >5%; threatens business model, licence or going concern |

Thresholds are illustrative — calibrate to the entity's financial-statement materiality and risk
appetite and record the basis. Assessment score = L × M (max 25). Proposed bands: ≥12 SRRO
included and prioritised; 6–11 included, monitor; ≤5 excluded with rationale (still reconsider
aggregate exposure, B23). Use the same horizons as S1-02.

### Step 4 — Determine material information (S1-04)
For each included SRRO, list candidate information (governance, strategy, financial effects,
risk management, metrics, targets) from IFRS S2/SASB/entity sources, then ask:
- Could omitting/misstating/obscuring it reasonably influence primary users' decisions? (S1.18)
- Quantitative factors: size relative to FS amounts; qualitative factors: regulatory breach, licence,
  reputation with lenders/buyers, related parties, trend reversal (B19–B22).
- Aggregation: several individually unlikely sources with a similar effect (e.g. supply disruption)
  may be material together (B23).
- Uncertain future events: amount, timing, uncertainty of cash flows; range and likelihood (B11, 79).
- Don't obscure: aggregate/disaggregate so material items stand out (B26–B27).
Conclude: material (disclose) / not material (omit, even if a standard specifies it — S1.B26 logic).

### Step 5 — Approve, document and reassess
Management approval and audit committee review; disclose the process (S1.44, judgements S1.74–76).
Reassess on significant event or change in circumstances (B11–B12): acquisition, new market,
regulation, extreme weather, financing covenant change.

## Systems view (risk multipliers)
Map how SRROs interact (e.g. drought → hydropower shortfall → power cuts → garment output; floods →
rice yields → MFI arrears → credit risk). Disclose combined effects where larger than single risks
(S1.21–24, S1.40(c), B23). Mark reinforcing loops and the one intervention that reduces several risks.

## Nature-related risks (TNFD LEAP)
S1 already requires material nature-related SRROs. Use LEAP — **Locate** interface with nature
(priority locations: sensitive ecosystems, water stress, protected areas, Indigenous/communal land),
**Evaluate** dependencies and impacts, **Assess** risks and opportunities and their financial effects,
**Prepare** responses and disclosure. TNFD's 14 recommended disclosures use the same four pillars (plus
Indigenous Peoples/local communities engagement and priority locations). The ISSB nature project
(exposure draft of a Practice Statement, Oct 2026, comments to 19 Feb 2027) is guidance, not a
requirement — check status before relying on it.

## Common errors
- Starting from a GRI "material topics" list and relabelling it.
- Scoring only likelihood; ignoring access to finance and cost of capital channels.
- Excluding value-chain risks because data is hard (use estimates and disclose).
- No link between SRRO register, ERM register and the financial-statement risk assessment.
