## 1. Scope, effective date and transition
- IFRS 18 replaces IAS 1. It sets general and specific requirements for presentation and disclosure. Recognition and measurement are unchanged.
- **C1:** effective for annual reporting periods beginning on or after **1 January 2027**. Earlier application is permitted and must be disclosed.
- **C2:** applied retrospectively under IAS 8, so comparatives are restated.
- **C3:** for the comparative period immediately preceding the period of first application, disclose a reconciliation, for each line item in the statement of profit or loss, between the amounts restated under IFRS 18 and the amounts previously presented under IAS 1.
- **C6:** an entity is permitted to present that reconciliation for the current period or earlier comparative periods as well.
- **C4 (verify):** relief from certain IAS 8.28(f) quantitative disclosures.
- **C5 (verify):** in the first year, condensed interim financial statements under IAS 34 present the IFRS 18 headings and required subtotals and give the C3 reconciliation.
- **C7–C8 (verify):** relate to certain elections, including investments in associates and joint ventures and IAS 8 interactions.
- Timeline for a December year end: 2026 is the comparative year, so 2026 transactions must be capturable in IFRS 18 categories. Settle classification, SMBA and MPM judgements in Q4 2026. The 2026 statement of profit or loss is restated, and IFRS 18 is mandatory from 1 January 2027.
- Third statement of financial position (**37–38**): required when a retrospective change has a material effect on the statement at the beginning of the preceding period. KPMG's illustrative group presents one, partly because of a separate correction of an error.

## 2. Roles, materiality and grouping
- **15–17, B8–B14:** the primary financial statements provide a *useful structured summary* of assets, liabilities, equity, income, expenses and cash flows. In judging that, consider whether the information gives an understandable overview, enables comparisons, and highlights items users may want to investigate in the notes. The notes provide *additional material information*.
- **19–20, B1–B5:** information that is not material need not be presented or disclosed, even if a Standard lists it as a requirement. Do not obscure material information with immaterial detail. IAS 8.27A–27I covers materiality for accounting policy disclosures, and Practice Statement 2 also applies.
- **24:** present additional line items, headings and subtotals when necessary for a useful structured summary.
- **41–43, B15–B23:** classify and aggregate items on the basis of *shared characteristics*. Disaggregate items with characteristics that are not shared when the resulting information is material. Characteristics include nature, function, measurement basis, size, location, and the type of customer or asset.
- **B24–B26:** use labels that faithfully represent the item. Avoid uninformative "other". When an aggregation labelled "other" is material, disclose information about its composition or use a more informative label, such as "Other operating expenses, including flood-related expenses".
- **44–45:** offset only when required or permitted.
- **31–35:** comparative information.
- **10–11:** titles of the statements may differ from those in IFRS 18.
- **12:** one-statement or two-statement presentation of P&L and OCI.

## 3. Statement of profit or loss: categories (47–68)
| Category | What goes in (no SMBA) | Ref |
|---|---|---|
| Operating | Residual: everything not in another category, including volatile and unusual items | 52 |
| Investing | (a) associates, JVs and unconsolidated subsidiaries (equity method always investing); (b) cash and cash equivalents; (c) assets generating a return individually and largely independently (investment property, debt and equity investments, net investment in a finance lease where not an SMBA) | 53–54, B42–B48 |
| Financing | (a) liabilities from transactions that involve only the raising of finance (loans, bonds, some redeemable preference shares): all income and expenses; (b) other liabilities (lease liabilities, net defined benefit liability, discounted provisions, contract liabilities with a significant financing component): only interest and effects of interest-rate changes identified under another Standard | 59–61, B49–B57 |
| Income taxes | Tax expense or income under IAS 12 | 67 |
| Discontinued operations | IFRS 5 | 68 |

Investing includes income generated (interest, dividends, rentals), fair value changes, gains and losses on derecognition, impairment, and incremental expenses directly attributable to acquiring or disposing of the asset (B47).

## 4. Specified main business activities (49–51, B30–B41)
- The two SMBAs are **investing in particular types of assets** (investment property owners, investment entities, insurers' investments) and **providing financing to customers** (banks, finance companies, finance lessors, manufacturers' captive finance).
- Whether an SMBA exists is a matter of fact, not an assertion. It needs judgement supported by evidence. Indicators include a reportable segment consisting of that single activity, and a subtotal such as gross margin or net interest margin being an important indicator of operating performance (B30–B41).
- Assess it at the reporting entity level. In consolidated statements, take the group as a whole: a subsidiary's activity can be a main business activity of the group.
- Consequences:
  - **Investing SMBA (55–58):** income and expenses from those assets move to operating. Equity-accounted results stay in investing.
  - **Financing SMBA (63–66):** income and expenses from liabilities relating to providing financing to customers go to operating. There is an accounting policy choice (**65**) to classify income and expenses from other financing liabilities in operating or financing. Income from cash and cash equivalents may also be operating (verify 56). The PBFIT subtotal may not be required if the entity classifies all financing in operating (verify 69(b), 73).
  - **IAS 7:** interest and dividends follow the P&L classification.
- A change in SMBA status is applied prospectively, with disclosure (verify 51–52).

## 5. Hard classification cases
- **FX differences (B65–B67):** classify them in the category of the income and expenses from the items that gave rise to them. FX on trade receivables and payables goes to operating, on investments to investing, and on borrowings to financing. If tracing involves undue cost or effort, classify in operating (verify B67). FX on intra-group balances that are not eliminated goes to operating, because there is no related P&L item (KPMG policy, IFRIC Update March 2026).
- **Derivatives (B70–B76):**
  - Designated hedges go in the category affected by the hedged risk, and ineffectiveness follows the effective portion (B71).
  - Derivatives used to manage identified risks but not designated go in the category affected by the risk managed, unless that would require grossing up gains and losses, in which case they go to operating (B72–B73).
  - Derivatives not used to manage identified risks go to investing. The exceptions are SMBA entities (operating) and derivatives relating to transactions that involve only raising finance (financing) (verify B74–B76).
- **Hybrid contracts (B77):** classification depends on whether the embedded derivative is separated.
- **Investment property:** KPMG's illustrative group reclassified rental income from revenue to investing, and fair value changes from other income to investing. It moved maintenance expense to administrative expenses (operating), which shows that costs not incremental to acquisition or disposal can stay in operating. Record this as a judgement.
- **Defined benefit plans:** net interest goes to financing (59(b), B52–B54). Service cost goes to operating.
- **Unwind of discount on provisions and lease interest:** financing.
- **Impairment of trade receivables (IFRS 9):** operating, and required as a separate line where applicable (75(b)(ii)). Impairment of debt investments is investing.
- **Gain on disposal of PPE:** operating (B79).
- **Remeasurement of a previously held equity interest in an acquiree:** investing (B78 additional line in KPMG).
- **Change in fair value of contingent consideration:** operating in KPMG. This is a judgement.
- **Hyperinflation net monetary gain or loss:** operating (verify B68–B69).

## 6. Subtotals and required line items
- **69:** (a) operating profit or loss; (b) profit or loss before financing and income taxes (exceptions for certain SMBA entities, 73 – verify); (c) profit or loss.
- **Additional subtotals (24, B78):** must fit the category structure. Gross profit is common. Do not insert a subtotal that straddles categories.
- **75(a):** revenue, with interest revenue under the effective interest method and insurance revenue presented separately where applicable; operating expenses (78–82); share of profit of equity-accounted investees (in investing); tax expense; a single amount for discontinued operations.
- **75(b):** further lines when material, for example impairment losses under IFRS 9 and gains and losses on derecognition of financial assets at amortised cost (verify the full list).
- **76:** attribution of profit to owners and NCI. **87:** attribution of total comprehensive income.
- Lines may be omitted if immaterial or if separate presentation would not give a useful structured summary (KPMG footnote, IFRS 18.19, 23, 41–43).

## 7. Operating expenses (78–85, B80–B85)
- **78:** classify and present operating expenses in the way that gives the most useful structured summary: by nature, by function, or a mixed presentation. Relevant factors include the drivers of profitability, how management runs the business, industry practice and how the entity reports internally (B80–B81). In a mixed presentation, some lines are by nature and others by function.
- **82:** when expenses are presented by function, describe what each function line includes, for example "Cost of sales comprises …".
- **83:** if *any* operating expenses are presented by function, disclose in a **single note**:
  - (a) the total of each of the following: **depreciation** (IAS 16, IFRS 16, and IAS 40 at cost), **amortisation** (IAS 38), **employee benefits** (IAS 19 and IFRS 2 services), **impairment losses and reversals** (IAS 36) and **inventory write-downs and reversals** (IAS 2);
  - (b)(i) for each, the amount relating to each function line item in the operating category;
  - (b)(ii) any line items outside operating that include such amounts, for example IAS 40 cost-model depreciation in investing.
- **83(a) total (KPMG and IFRIC Update March 2026):** the total for the period includes amounts capitalised, such as depreciation into inventory or employee benefits into development costs. For each function line, the entity may disclose amounts incurred (expensed plus capitalised), explaining qualitatively that capitalised amounts are included and identifying the assets. The alternative is to disclose only the amounts recognised as an expense (KPMG Appendix IV).
- **84–85:** no further by-nature disaggregation is required unless another Standard requires it or it is material.

## 8. Other comprehensive income (86–95)
- **86:** totals for OCI and total comprehensive income.
- **88:** two groups: items that will not be reclassified to P&L, and items that will be reclassified when specific conditions are met.
- **89:** the share of OCI of equity-accounted investees, split between those two groups.
- **90–92:** reclassification adjustments, presented in the statement or disclosed in the notes.
- **93–95:** present components either before tax with aggregate tax per group, or net of tax. Disclose the tax for each component.

## 9. Statement of financial position and changes in equity
- **96–101:** current and non-current classification, or liquidity order when that is more relevant. The criteria carry over from IAS 1.
- **103:** line items (a) to (s): PPE, investment property, intangible assets, **goodwill (new separate line, 103(d))**, financial assets, equity-accounted investments, biological assets, inventories, trade and other receivables, cash and cash equivalents, held-for-sale assets, trade and other payables, provisions, financial liabilities, current tax, deferred tax, and liabilities of disposal groups.
- **104:** non-controlling interests, and issued capital and reserves attributable to owners. **B109–B111:** additional lines based on dissimilar characteristics.
- **107–111:** the statement of changes in equity, carried over from IAS 1.
- **113–115, B112:** notes presented systematically and cross-referenced to the statements.
- **126–132 (verify):** share capital, reserves, dividends and other disclosures carried over from IAS 1.

## 10. Management-defined performance measures (117–125, B113–B141)
**Definition (117):** a subtotal of income and expenses that (a) is used in public communications outside the financial statements, (b) communicates management's view of an aspect of the financial performance of the entity as a whole, and (c) is not excluded by 118.

**Excluded (118, verify list):**
- subtotals specified by IFRS (operating profit, PBFIT, profit, profit from continuing operations, OCI, TCI);
- gross profit and similar;
- operating profit before depreciation, amortisation and impairments within the scope of IAS 36;
- operating profit plus income and expenses from equity-accounted investments;
- profit before tax.

A measure exactly equal to one of these is not an MPM. EBITDA computed as operating profit before D&A and IAS 36 impairment is therefore not an MPM, but "adjusted EBITDA" usually is.

**Other points:**
- Non-financial measures, ratios, cash-flow measures and items such as revenue growth are not MPMs.
- The numerator or denominator of a ratio can be an MPM (B117).
- **Rebuttable presumption (119–120):** a subtotal used in public communications is presumed to communicate management's view. Rebutting it needs reasonable and supportable evidence.
- **Single note (121):** all MPM information goes in one note, and it is within audit scope. Presenting an MPM on the face of the P&L is allowed only if it meets the requirements for additional subtotals (B118). The note is still required.

**Disclosures (122–124):**
- 122: a statement that MPMs give management's view and are not necessarily comparable.
- 123(a): the aspect of performance communicated and why the measure is useful.
- 123(b): how it is calculated.
- 123(c): a reconciliation to the most directly comparable 118 subtotal or IFRS total or subtotal.
- 123(d): the income tax effect and the NCI effect for each reconciling item.
- 123(e): how the tax effect was determined (B141: the statutory rate in the relevant jurisdiction, a reasonable pro-rata allocation of current and deferred tax, or another more appropriate method).
- 124: changes to MPMs, with the reasons and restated comparatives.
- B137(b): state the line item in which each reconciling item is presented.

**KPMG illustrative example:**

| | 2026 | 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Operating profit | 9,145 | 8,838 |
| MPM adjustments (impairment reversals of PPE, intangibles and inventory, goodwill impairment, disposal-group remeasurement, flood costs) | + | + |
| Adjusted operating profit | 9,312 | 10,288 |

The tax effect is at the statutory rate, and each item shows an NCI effect. Adjusted operating profit is also the numerator of return on capital. The comparatives use the current-year MPM definition because no MPMs were identified before adoption.

## 11. Consequential amendments
- **IAS 7:**
  - The indirect method starts from **operating profit or loss** (18(b), 20). The reconciling items change accordingly: there is no add-back of finance costs or tax, and there are adjustments for FX in operating and so on.
  - Interest and dividends (33A–34D, verify numbering): the policy choice is removed for entities without an SMBA. Interest paid and dividends paid are financing; interest received and dividends received are investing.
  - For SMBA entities, classification follows the P&L. An entity that classifies these in more than one P&L category chooses a single cash-flow category (verify).
- **IAS 33.73B–73C:** additional per-share amounts may be disclosed only if the numerator is a total or subtotal identified in IFRS 18 or an MPM, with reconciliations. KPMG example: adjusted operating profit per share.
- **IAS 34:** interim reports include MPM disclosures. In the first year of application, the IFRS 18 headings and subtotals and the C3 reconciliation also apply.
- **IAS 8:** renamed *Basis of Preparation of Financial Statements*. It now holds going concern, accrual basis, fair presentation and compliance, consistency, and accounting policy materiality (27A–27I) moved from IAS 1.

## 12. Initial application disclosure (KPMG Note 5 pattern)
1. A statement that IFRS 18 was applied from date X, retrospectively, replacing IAS 1, with neither net profit nor net assets changed.
2. (i) The reconciliation of the comparative P&L in IAS 1 vs adjustment vs IFRS 18 columns, line by line (C3), with lettered explanations. In KPMG's case:
   - **A** investment property moved to investing;
   - **B** DB-plan interest from cost of sales to financing;
   - **C** FX by source;
   - **D** financial-asset income to investing;
   - **E** CFH ineffectiveness follows the hedged item;
   - **F** economic hedges follow the risk;
   - **G** equity-accounted share to investing.
3. (ii) The MPM identified, with a cross-reference to the MPM note.
4. (iii) The aggregation and disaggregation changes (for example, the goodwill line) and the relabelling of "other".
5. (iv) The IAS 7 changes: starting point is now operating profit, and interest paid is reclassified from operating to financing.

## 13. Implementation process (KPMG East Africa deck)
1. **Assess:** map every P&L line to a category and test for an SMBA.
2. **Redesign:** the P&L layout, the chart of accounts and mapping.
3. **Implement:** MPM governance and controls, system and data changes, team training.
4. **Report:** restated comparatives, MPM reconciliations and disclosure notes.

Same underlying economics, a different way of telling the performance story. The performance measures an entity communicates publicly now come under audit.
