# Sustainability reporting requirements: review and analysis (IFRS S1 and IFRS S2, Cambodian context)

Prepared November 2026 from five source documents:

1. *Professional Certificate of Sustainability Reporting — Cambodia (IFRS S1 & S2)*, programme syllabus,
   zero draft, 1 November 2026.
2. PwC, *Sustainability reporting guide* (SRG), chapters 1–5, as of September 2026.
3. ACLEDA Bank Plc., *Sustainability Report 2025*.
4. UN SSE / TNFD, *Model Guidance on Nature-related Financial Disclosures*, v2025.A.
5. Bissoondoyal-Bheenick et al., "ESG capital and corporate value creation", *Applied Economics*, 2026.

---

## 1. The requirement architecture

| Layer | Instrument | Status and what it demands |
|---|---|---|
| Global baseline | **IFRS S1** (June 2023) | General requirements: disclose material information about **all** sustainability-related risks and opportunities (SRROs) that could reasonably be expected to affect cash flows, access to finance or cost of capital (S1.3). Four pillars, plus connectivity, judgements and comparatives. |
| Global thematic | **IFRS S2** (June 2023) and the **December 2025 GHG amendments** (effective 1 January 2027) | Climate: governance, strategy, transition plan, financial effects, scenario-based resilience, risk management, Scope 1–3 GHG, the S2.29(b)–(g) metrics, targets and carbon credits. The amendments add reliefs for Cat 15, industry classification, jurisdictional measurement methods and GWPs. |
| Guidance hierarchy | SASB / Industry-based Guidance ("shall refer to and consider"), CDSB, GRI/ESRS (for information only), ISSB educational material, TIG | Identify industry SRROs and metrics. GRI and ESRS may shape *what information* to give, but they cannot be used to identify SRROs (S1.BC136–137). |
| Assurance and ethics | ISSA 5000; IESBA sustainability revisions and IESSA (both effective 15 December 2026) | Limited or reasonable assurance. Ethics duties for preparers (s.220, s.270) and independence rules for assurers. |
| Nature (emerging) | TNFD (14 recommended disclosures, LEAP); ISSB nature Practice Statement exposure draft (October 2026) | Nature risks are already in scope under S1 where material. The Practice Statement would be guidance, not a requirement. |
| Cambodia | **SERC Prakas N.034 (10 May 2024)**: 31 ESG items for listed entities from 2026. ACAR adoption of ISSB not yet confirmed. NBC sustainable finance principles. | A local, TCFD-shaped template. ISSB application is assumed voluntary (syllabus premise) until ACAR, SERC or NBC act. |

**Key point.** IFRS S1 is the operating system and IFRS S2 is its first application. The disclosure
pillars are identical; S2 adds prescribed calculations (GHG) and analysis (scenarios). So a reporting
*process* should be designed once around S1 and then extended for climate. The two skills follow
this design.

## 2. Analysis by requirement area

### 2.1 Boundary, period and time horizons (SRG 3; S1.20, 30, 64–66)
- The reporting entity is the same as in the financial statements, with 100% of consolidated subsidiaries.
  S1 has no specific guidance on joint operations or leases, so the general principle is applied and the
  judgement documented.
- Investments held under fiduciary duty are **not** excluded under ISSB, unlike under ESRS.
- Time horizons are entity-specific but must be linked to planning and capital-allocation cycles. This
  flexibility is unique to ISSB, and reviewers do check the link.
- Disclosures are published **at the same time as the financial statements** (S1.64). Year-1 relief
  (S1.E4) only defers this.

### 2.2 Materiality (SRG 4; S1.17–19, B1–B37)
- There are two steps: (1) identify SRROs that could reasonably affect prospects; (2) apply materiality
  to the *information* about them. S1 never calls a risk "material".
- S1 does not require impact materiality. Impacts matter only when they create or explain an SRRO.
  Mixing GRI impact scores into the ISSB conclusion is the most common methodological error (it is also
  a syllabus learning point, LO4).
- No method is prescribed. SRG suggests likelihood × magnitude, taking account of completed mitigation
  but not planned mitigation. A risk can be "reasonably expected" from the users' point of view even
  where management disagrees.
- Aggregation (B23): several unlikely sources of a similar effect can be material together. This supports
  the syllabus's "risk multiplier" systems view (Case E).

### 2.3 Foundations (SRG 5; S1.11–16, 21–24, 72, 74–86)
- The fair-presentation and qualitative characteristics are aligned with ESRS. Timeliness is ISSB-only.
- **Proportionality** works through two mechanisms: "reasonable and supportable information without
  undue cost or effort" and "commensurate with skills, capabilities and resources". Both relieve effort,
  not the obligation. The more useful the information, the more effort is expected (BC17).
- **Connectivity**: data and assumptions must be consistent with the financial statements as far as
  possible (S1.23). This is where accountants add most value: impairment (IAS 36), useful lives (IAS 16),
  provisions (IAS 37), biological assets (IAS 41) and ECL (IFRS 9).
- **Compliance statement**: it must be explicit and unreserved, and only full compliance qualifies
  (S1.72). Partial or voluntary reporters must describe their reporting differently (ISSB guide,
  September 2024).
- Commercial-sensitivity relief covers **opportunities only** (S1.B34–B37). ESRS relief is wider.

### 2.4 Climate (IFRS S2; syllabus sessions 5–10)
- **Scenario analysis** must be proportionate, not optional. The syllabus maps S&P's quality ladder
  (levels 0–4) onto S2.22, and its 10-point checklist is the practical test. About 40% of companies
  globally report no climate risk assessment, and about 50% of financial institutions. The gap is large
  everywhere.
- **GHG**: the syllabus's six-step process improves on the usual five steps because it adds boundary
  setting at the start and review at the end. Recurring errors: applying GWP twice, AR4 calculator GWPs,
  UK biofuel-blend diesel, purchases treated as consumption, stale grid factors.
- **Cross-industry metrics** S2.29(b)–(g) (vulnerable and aligned amounts, capital deployment, carbon
  price, remuneration) are often left out of first reports. They need finance-team data, not
  sustainability-team data.

## 3. Practice evidence: ACLEDA Bank 2025 compared with ISSB

ACLEDA is Cambodia's first listed bank. It prepared its 2025 report voluntarily against Prakas 034 (31
items mapped), ahead of the 2026 mandate.

| Area | What ACLEDA discloses | ISSB gap | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Framework | Prakas 034 and SDG mapping; "national and international best practices" | No ISSB compliance statement (S1.72); SASB industry not considered (S1.55) | High |
| Materiality | 9 "materiality risk factors" prioritised | Unclear whether the lens is financial (ISSB) or impact; horizons not linked to planning (S1.30) | High |
| Strategy | ESG integrated into credit assessment | No current or anticipated financial effects (S2.15–21); **no scenario analysis or resilience assessment** (S2.22) | High |
| GHG | Scope 1: 3,155 t; Scope 2: 21,100 t (group); Scope 3: Cat 6 air travel only (bank only); 2023 Scope 2 restated after switching to the Ministry of Environment grid factor | **No Cat 15 financed emissions**, normally the dominant category for a bank (S2.29(a)(vi)(2), B58–B63). Restatement needs S1.83–86/B52 disclosure. Boundary for Scope 3 differs from Scope 1–2. Consolidation approach and GWP basis not stated. | High |
| Metrics | Intensity per FTE; environmental and social indicators | S2.29(b)–(g) missing; PCAF data-quality scores missing | Medium |
| Assurance | "Has not engaged an independent third-party provider" | Not ready for ISSA 5000; no evidence index | Medium |
| Governance | ESG governance described | Skills, frequency and remuneration links (S1.27; S2.6) need checking | Medium |

**Teaching insight:** ACLEDA's Scope 2 note shows that a Ministry of Environment grid factor is already
in use by at least one Cambodian reporter. The syllabus open item ("official Cambodian grid factor to
replace IFI 2021 0.5878") may therefore be closer to resolution than assumed. Obtain the MoE value and
its year before the first intake.

## 4. Cross-document observations

1. **The syllabus is strong on the control layer.** Its GHG-00 to GHG-09 and MA-01 to MA-03
   working-paper set, maker-checker-approver, EDQA scoring and ICSR scorecard go beyond most ISSB
   guides, which say little about evidence. This is a differentiator for an accountant-led programme.
2. **The SRG fills interpretive gaps that the syllabus does not cover**: leases and joint operations in
   the boundary, fiduciary investments, subsequent events, 52/53-week periods, the "pick and choose"
   comparatives risk, and the limits on commercial sensitivity. These now appear in the S1 skill.
3. **Nature is the next frontier.** TNFD's LEAP and 14 disclosures use the same four pillars. The ISSB
   is converging on them (TNFD MoU April 2025; nature standard-setting decision November 2025; Practice
   Statement exposure draft October 2026). Cambodian rubber, rice and cassava cases (forest landscapes,
   Tonle Sap, Indigenous communal land) are natural LEAP cases.
4. **Why it matters financially.** The *Applied Economics* study finds ESG performance relates
   **non-linearly** to operating value. The largest gains come from moving from low to mid-range ESG,
   with diminishing returns after that. ESG also complements financial, intellectual and manufacturing
   capital. For Cambodian first-time reporters this argues for building the basics first (boundary, GHG
   Scope 1–2, controls, material SRROs) rather than for an exhaustive first report, and for presenting
   sustainability spend with the business case (NPV, payback, cost per tCO2e avoided), as the capstone
   requires.
5. **The regulatory landscape is fragmented but converging.** ESRS (2026) has been simplified and
   narrowed by the Omnibus; ISSB adoption covers 39 jurisdictions with profiles. For a Cambodian exporter
   the practical demand comes from EU buyers, lenders and DFIs as much as from SERC.

## 4a. Cambodia carbon pricing and ETS (addendum)
- **Status:** Cambodia has no ETS or carbon tax in force. A carbon-market sub-decree is an NDC 3.0
  milestone ("by 2026"), and its approval is not confirmed. An Article 6 framework is operating: a
  Ministry of Environment Operations Manual, the Carbon Crediting Secretariat, eligible mechanisms (JCM,
  Gold Standard, VCS, A6.4), letters of authorisation already issued, and forestry credits restricted
  from export.
- **How to treat it under ISSB:** a *possible* scheme is a policy/legal transition risk (S2.10). It
  should be modelled as a scenario (CR-06), and the cost ranges disclosed as anticipated financial effects
  (S2.15–21). If management uses the price paths internally, they are an internal carbon price
  (S2.29(f)). Credits go in CR-07 and are disclosed under S2.36(e), never netted against emissions.
- **Example result:** a garment SME sits far below a plausible coverage threshold. Its exposure is
  indirect, through electricity-tariff pass-through and buyer pricing (USD 11k–91k cumulative over
  2027–2035 across scenarios for Mekong Garment). Cement, brick kilns, power, steel and banks, through
  their borrowers, face direct exposure.
- **Accounting:** no IFRS covers allowances specifically, so a policy choice is needed under IAS 8
  (IAS 38/IAS 2 for allowances; IAS 37 for the obligation). The assumptions must be consistent with
  the financial statements (S1.23).

## 5. Implications for the reporting process (built into the skills)

| Principle | How the skills implement it |
|---|---|
| One process, two standards | `ifrs-s1-reporting` runs the 8-phase backbone; `ifrs-s2-reporting` adds 10 climate phases and calls back to S1 working papers |
| Investor lens kept separate from impact lens | S1-03 keeps impact materiality in a memo column; bands are driven by L × M only |
| Every figure traceable | S1-13 and GHG-09 evidence indices; GHG-02 single factor register with INDEX/MATCH lookups |
| Proportionality made explicit | SA1 proportionality assessment; S1-06 and CR-02 force a "reason if not quantitative" |
| Connectivity | S1-09 lists FS line item, judgement and assumption side by side, with a consistency flag |
| Assurance readiness | S1-12 controls matrix, S1-14 review log and ICSR maturity scorecard with bands |
| Cambodia layer | Prakas 034 → ISSB mapping, local data sources, sector SRRO prompts, open-items list |

## 6. Open items to confirm (carried into the skills)

- ACAR/NAA adoption of IFRS S1/S2 and ISSA 5000, including effective dates.
- Whether SERC or NBC will require ISSB for listed issuers and banks.
- The official Ministry of Environment/EAC grid factor and its year.
- NDC 3.0 base year and the conditional/unconditional split.
- The status of Article 6 arrangements (JCM; Cambodia–Singapore).
- The exact wording of the December 2025 S2 amendments (Cat 15 limits, GICS alternative, jurisdictional
  reliefs), to be checked against the published text.
